Unit 7 — Electrical Systems

Explore how electricity, components, and circuits work together in real engineering systems. You'll move from basic circuit theory to building and troubleshooting circuits that power the devices you've already built.

~3–4 Weeks Hands-On Lab 7 Lessons
Lesson 7.1 Week 1
Using a Multimeter
  • Voltage, current, and resistance measurement
  • Reading the DMM display and selecting ranges
  • Polarity and probe placement
  • Lab: measure battery voltage and resistor values
Live
Lesson 7.2 Week 1
Breadboards & Circuit Assembly
  • How breadboard rows, columns, and rails connect internally
  • Reading schematic symbols and translating to hardware
  • Avoiding short circuits and power-rail mistakes
  • Lab: build your first circuit from a schematic
Live
Lesson 7.3 Week 1
LED Circuits — Illumination Creation
  • LED forward voltage, forward current, and polarity
  • Why every LED needs a current-limiting resistor
  • Calculating R from supply voltage and LED specs
  • Lab: light multiple LEDs in the same circuit
Live
Lesson 7.4 Week 2
Ohm's Law
  • V = I × R — solving for any unknown variable
  • Units: Volts, Amperes, Ohms
  • Proportional relationships: more R → less I (same V)
  • Lab: verify Ohm's Law experimentally with multimeter
Live
Lesson 7.5 Week 2
Series vs. Parallel Circuits
  • Series: Rtotal = R₁ + R₂ + … ; current is shared
  • Parallel: 1/Rtotal = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + … ; voltage is shared
  • Why household circuits are wired in parallel
  • Lab: measure voltage and current in both topologies
Live
Lesson 7.6 Week 3
Equivalent Resistance
  • Identify series groups and parallel groups in a network
  • Step-by-step reduction to find Req
  • Mixed circuits: series within parallel (and vice versa)
  • Lab: predict and verify Req for multi-resistor networks
Live
Lesson 7.7 Week 3–4
Kirchhoff's Laws
  • KCL — currents into a node must equal currents out
  • KVL — voltage rises and drops around any loop sum to zero
  • Applying both laws to analyze multi-loop circuits
  • Lab: trace voltage and current through a complex circuit
Live
