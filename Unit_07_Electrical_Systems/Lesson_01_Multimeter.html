Parts of a Digital Multimeter

  • 🔢 Digital Display — reads the measured value; auto-ranging models pick the right scale automatically. DMM = Digital Multimeter
  • 🎛️ Dial / Selector — choose what you're measuring: DC voltage (V⎓), AC voltage (V~), DC current (A⎓), or resistance (Ω). Always set the dial BEFORE connecting probes.
  • 🔴 Red Probe — the positive lead (V, Ω, mA/µA jack). Goes into the V·Ω jack for voltage and resistance; into the A jack for current.
  • ⚫ Black Probe — the negative lead, always goes into the COM (common) jack. COM = Common Ground.
  • ⚡ 10A Jack — a separate jack for measuring large currents only. Move the red probe here when measuring currents that might exceed 200 mA. Most circuits you'll measure are well under 200 mA.

Measuring Voltage (DC)

  1. Set dial to DC Voltage (V⎓).
  2. Plug black probe → COM jack; red probe → V·Ω jack.
  3. Connect in parallel across the component — one probe on each side of it.
  4. Read the display. A positive reading means current would flow from red to black through the component.
  5. If the display shows a negative value, swap your probes (you had polarity reversed).
Key rule: Voltmeters connect in parallel. Never insert a voltmeter in series — it looks like an open circuit to the rest of the components.

Measuring Current (DC)

  1. Turn off the circuit before reconnecting probes.
  2. Set dial to DC Current (A⎓ or mA).
  3. Move red probe → A or mA jack (check which range you need).
  4. Break the circuit at the point you want to measure — disconnect one wire.
  5. Insert the ammeter in that break: the circuit now flows through the meter.
  6. Reconnect power and read the display.
Key rule: Ammeters connect in series. Never connect an ammeter in parallel across a component — it looks like a short circuit and will blow the meter's fuse.

Measuring Resistance (Ω)

  1. De-energize the circuit completely — remove the battery or unplug.
  2. Set dial to Resistance (Ω).
  3. Plug black → COM; red → V·Ω jack.
  4. Place probes across the component (parallel, same as voltage).
  5. Read the resistance value. Use the resistor's color band code to verify the reading makes sense.
⚠️ Never measure resistance in a live circuit. The meter supplies its own tiny test current to measure resistance. External voltage will give wrong readings and can damage the meter.

Range Settings & Auto-Ranging

Manual Range

  • Start at the highest range for an unknown value
  • Reduce range until you get the most significant digits
  • Example: set 20V range to measure a 9V battery
  • "OL" or "1" on the display = overload — go to a higher range

Auto-Range

  • The meter selects the range automatically
  • Convenient but may be slower to settle on a reading
  • Use manual range when you need faster response or know the approximate value
Prefix reminder: 1 kΩ = 1,000 Ω · 1 mA = 0.001 A · 1 MΩ = 1,000,000 Ω

Safety Rules

  • Never exceed the rated input. Most DMMs handle 600V DC max — check the label. Exceeding it destroys the meter.
  • Inspect probes before use. Cracked insulation can cause shock. Replace damaged probes immediately.
  • Never measure resistance in a live circuit. External voltage gives wrong readings and damages the meter.
  • Never leave the meter in current mode (A jack) when measuring voltage — it's a direct short circuit.
  • Set the dial first, then connect probes. Connecting first in the wrong mode can blow the meter's internal fuse.
  • When in doubt about range, start high. Always safer to go down than blow the fuse.

Lab: Measure What You Can Find

Today's Measurements

  • Measure the voltage of your 9V battery (DC voltage mode)
  • Measure the voltage of your AA battery (DC voltage mode)
  • Read the color bands on 3 resistors, calculate expected Ω, then measure actual Ω
  • Calculate percent error: |measured − expected| / expected × 100%
