Part B · Solve It 4 pts each · 20 pts

  1. A 12 V battery is connected across a 600 Ω resistor. What is the current?
    I = V ÷ R = 12 V ÷ 600 Ω → I = 0.02 A (20 mA)
  2. 1.5 A flows through an 8 Ω speaker coil. What voltage is across it?
    V = I × R = 1.5 A × 8 Ω → V = 12 V
  3. A 9 V motor draws 0.3 A. What is its resistance?
    R = V ÷ I = 9 V ÷ 0.3 A → R = 30 Ω
  4. 15 mA flows through a 330 Ω resistor. What voltage is across the resistor?
    15 mA = 0.015 A · V = 0.015 A × 330 Ω → V = 4.95 V
  5. A 5 V supply is connected to a 2.2 kΩ resistor. What is the current in mA? (Round to two decimal places.)
    2.2 kΩ = 2,200 Ω · I = 5 V ÷ 2,200 Ω = 0.002273 A → I ≈ 2.27 mA
Suggested scoring (4 pts): 1 correct formula · 1 values plugged in, converted to A / V / Ω · 1 correct number · 1 correct unit.
Common mistake: skipping the conversion (#4: 15 × 330 = 4,950 V; #5: 5 ÷ 2.2 = 2.27 A). Give the formula point, not the number point.

Part C · LED Resistor 6 pts

R = (Vsupply − Vf) ÷ I  ·  Round up to the next standard value: 100, 120, 150, 180, 220, 270, 330, 360, 390, 470, 560, 680 Ω
  1. A green LED (Vf = 2.2 V) runs at 20 mA from a 5 V supply. Calculate R, then pick the standard resistor.
    R = (5 V − 2.2 V) ÷ 0.02 A = 2.8 V ÷ 0.02 A = 140 Ω → R = 140 Ω · use 150 Ω
Suggested scoring (6 pts): 1 subtracts Vf (2.8 V) · 1 converts 20 mA → 0.02 A · 2 correct R = 140 Ω with unit · 2 picks 150 Ω.
Picking 120 Ω (rounding down) loses the last 2 pts — it would push the LED above 20 mA.
