L7.2 Using a Multimeter

Mastering the Digital Multimeter (DMM): Probes, Mode Selection, Range, and Circuit Connections.

📟 Core Functions

  • Voltmeter (V⎓): Measure electrical potential — connect in parallel
  • Ammeter (A⎓ / mA): Measure charge flow rate — connect in series
  • Ohmmeter (Ω): Measure component resistance — circuit de-energized
  • Safety Rules: Protect meter fuses and prevent short circuits

Parallel Connection

Measuring Voltage (DC V⎓)

Mode: Parallel Circuit Connection
  1. Set dial to DC Voltage (V⎓).
  2. Plug Black → COM; Red → V·Ω jack.
  3. Connect in PARALLEL across the component (one probe on each terminal).
  4. Positive display = current flows Red → Black. Negative = probes reversed.
Why Parallel? Internal voltmeter resistance is extremely high (~10 MΩ). Inserting it in series would block circuit current completely!
Series Connection

Measuring Current (DC A⎓)

Mode: Series (In-Line) Connection
  1. Turn OFF power before connecting probes.
  2. Set dial to DC Current (mA or 10A) and move Red probe to matching current jack.
  3. BREAK the circuit path and insert probes in-line so current passes through the meter.
  4. Power on circuit and take reading.
⚠️ NEVER connect in parallel! An ammeter has nearly zero resistance. Parallel contact creates a direct short circuit and blows the internal meter fuse!
De-energized Testing

Measuring Resistance (Ω)

Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. De-energize completely: Disconnect batteries and power plugs.
  2. Set dial to Resistance (Ω).
  3. Red probe in V·Ω jack, Black probe in COM.
  4. Touch probe tips across the isolated component.
⚠️ Never Measure Live Resistance!
The DMM injects its own micro-current signal to compute R = V / I. Live voltage from an external circuit corrupts readings and can destroy meter components.

Meter Protection

Golden Safety Rules

1. Pre-Flight Dial Verification

Always set the dial to the correct function BEFORE touching probes to live circuit terminals.

2. Post-Current Reset

Immediately after taking current measurements, move the Red probe out of the 10A/mA jack back to the V·Ω jack.

3. High-Range Default Rule

When using a manual-ranging DMM with unknown voltages or currents, start at the highest range scale and step down to avoid over-range fuse damage.
