L7.4 LED Circuits: Illumination Creation

Course: Unit 7 Electrical Systems · Robotics / Engineering (Grades 9–12)

Topics: Forward Voltage (Vf) · Current-Limiting Resistors · Series vs. Parallel

Learning Objectives:

  • Identify LED anode and cathode using physical methods.
  • Calculate current-limiting resistor values using Ohm's Law.
  • Compare circuit dynamics in series vs. parallel LED configurations.

What is an LED?

LED = Light Emitting Diode

  • A semiconductor diode that emits light when current flows in one direction (Anode → Cathode).
  • Acts like an electrical check valve: blocks reverse current flow.
  • Current-Driven: Brightness increases with current, but excessive current will instantly burn out the component.
  • Standard 5mm LEDs operate safely at 10 mA to 20 mA.

Identifying Anode (+) & Cathode (−)

Anode (+) — Positive

  • Longer Lead
  • Smaller internal wire post
  • Connects towards higher supply voltage

Cathode (−) — Negative

  • Shorter Lead ("Clipped = Cathode")
  • Flat edge on the plastic casing
  • Larger internal anvil flag
  • Connects towards Ground (GND)

Forward Voltage (Vf) & Forward Current (If)

Forward Voltage (Vf): The voltage drop across the LED junction required for it to conduct.

  • 🔴 Red LED: ≈ 1.8 V – 2.0 V
  • 🟢 Green LED: ≈ 2.0 V – 2.4 V
  • 🔵 Blue / White LED: ≈ 3.0 V – 3.4 V

Forward Current (If): Target operating current (typically 0.020 A / 20 mA).

Calculating Current-Limiting Resistors

Formula based on Ohm's Law (R = V ÷ I):

R = (Vsupply − Vf) ÷ If

Wiring Multiple LEDs

Series Wiring

  • Voltage requirement accumulates: Vtotal = Vf1 + Vf2 + …
  • Uses one resistor for the chain.
  • If one LED fails, the entire loop opens.

Parallel Wiring

  • Each LED requires its own individual resistor.
  • Never share a single resistor across parallel LEDs (causes thermal runaway / current hogging).
  • If one LED fails, the remaining branches continue functioning.
