Professional Ethics

You can apply ethical principles to guide decisions in your personal and professional daily life. However, because each of us has a different personal definition of what is moral and ethical, professional practices require the use of a more consistent code of ethics. 

TermDefinition
ethical principles
A system of moral values that everyone accepts as good human behavior.  

The Purpose of a Code

A code of ethics offers rules for behavior that can help professionals make difficult ethical decisions when faced with complex problems. An accepted ethical code can provide a measure of protection for consumers, but it also offers a measure of defense for professionals who follow the code if something goes wrong. 

Many engineering societies have developed a code of ethics to define ethical standards for engineering practice and guide ethical behavior even though individual engineers may hold different moral beliefs. Eight fundamental canons are common to most engineering codes of ethics.

TermDefinition
canon
A general law, rule, principle, or criterion by which something is judged. 

Fundamental Engineering Canons

  1. Hold paramount the safety, health, and welfare of the public.

  2. Perform services only in areas of competence.

  3. Issue public statements only in an objective and truthful manner.

  4. Act for each employer or client as a faithful agent or trustee.

  5. Avoid deceptive acts.

  6. Conduct yourself honorably, responsibly, ethically, and lawfully so as to enhance the honor, reputation, and usefulness of the profession.

  7. Continue professional development.

  8. Treat all persons fairly.

Many ethical decisions are complex, and the codes do not always offer a straightforward approach to addressing an ethical dilemma. Engineers must be conscientious and think critically when making ethical decisions. See Ethical Decision-Making on the Principles of Ethics(opens in a new tab) resource. 

Ethical codes are moral, not legal, obligations. Professional associations expect their members to abide by these codes. A professional engineer can lose their license if found to have violated the code of ethics. 

NOTE: More details about the eight fundamental engineering canons can be found in individual professional associations' codes of ethics. You can examine a code by visiting the website of a national engineering association, such as the National Society of Professional Engineers or the American Society of Civil Engineers, among others.
