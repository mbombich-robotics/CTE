Principles of Ethics

Ethics refers to moral values that affect how a person thinks and behaves. Personal moral values give each person a sense of what is right and wrong and guide them when making decisions. No matter where you go or what you do, you will face ethical dilemmas—in school, at work, at home, and with friends. The decisions you make to resolve these dilemmas may affect only a small number of people. Other decisions can have a much greater impact on people and the planet.

TermDefinition
moralA sense of what is right and wrong and guide them when making decisions.
ethical dilemmas A situation in which there is a choice to be made between two (or more) options, neither of which resolves the situation in an ethically acceptable fashion.  
ethical principles
Defining a system of moral values that everyone accepts as good human behavior.  

Deciding what is ethically acceptable to you often means considering ideas of honesty, fairness, and concern about others and the environment. But not everyone agrees about what is right and fair. Defining a system of moral values or ethical principles that everyone agrees on is not easy. 

  • Ethical Dilemmas in Society

    • Genome editing. Should we edit our DNA?

    • Personal privacy. Should we protect online privacy?

    • Food security. Who should be responsible for ensuring all people have access to healthy food?

    • Global population growth. Should we impose population controls?

    • Artificial intelligence. Should we give computers the right to make medical decisions?

Ethics Start with Integrity 

Based on your life experience, you have already built a sense of what you believe is right and wrong, fair and just. You have developed personal habits related to telling the truth and keeping your promises. In other words, you have developed an internal conscience that guides you in making decisions.

Having personal integrity means acting in line with your internal conscience. Integrity includes being truthful, reliable, responsible, and empathetic. You show your integrity in all aspects of your life—at school, at home, on teams, and at work.

TermDefinition
integrityAct in a way that is consistent with your internal conscience even if it is not the best decision for you personally.   

Ethical Perspectives

It is important to remember that even people with high moral integrity may disagree on what is right and wrong. The way you perceive and approach an ethical dilemma is your ethical perspective. Your family and friends, culture, religion, where you live, and your personal experiences all affect your ethical perspective. 

Understanding your own ethical perspective will help you explain and justify your position. Your ethical perspective can come from a range of life experiences and can change based on the situation. Below are a few common ethical perspectives. You may align closely with one or a combination of these, or apply different perspectives depending on the problem. 

Common Ethical Perspectives

Treat others equally and fairly. Respect others and their rights.

Be a good person. Be honest, courageous, and compassionate and forgive others.

Find solutions in which the positive impacts outweigh the negative consequences.

Take responsibility to reduce pain and suffering and protect the vulnerable. Behave in a way that strengthens personal relationships.

Ethical Discussions

A moral decision made by a person from one perspective will not necessarily be the same decision made by a person from another perspective. To develop good solutions to ethical dilemmas, you need to be able to consider other people’s perspectives. Understanding the ethical values of others will help you communicate and empathize with others.

People often feel strongly about their ethical position. When discussing ethical issues, it is a good idea to follow norms to promote a collaborative atmosphere. Always approach ethical discussions with the goal of understanding other perspectives, not with promoting your own.

Norms for Ethical Discussion

  • An ethical discussion is not a competition. There are no winners or losers.

  • Everyone has an equal opportunity to express their views.

  • Everyone will respect the different viewpoints of others.

  • Do not interrupt others. However, no one person should dominate the discussion.

  • Resolve conflicts in a manner that respects all participants.

  • Feedback will address ideas and not the person presenting the ideas.

Ethical Decisions  

When you face an ethical dilemma, step back and view the dilemma objectively. A decision-making process (Figure 1) can help you think through choices and make the best decision. Documenting your thought process will help you justify your decision. Remember that situations change. When a change occurs, rethink your decision to be sure that it is still the best choice available.
