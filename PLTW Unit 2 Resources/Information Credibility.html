Information Credibility

What Is Good Information?

Today’s information accessibility and abundance are higher than ever before. With so much information at our fingertips, checking the credibility of the information is more vital. How can you trust what you read online? 

Top-Level Domain

One of the key clues to the level of credibility is the top-level domain of the website where you find the information. Let’s take a look at the overall topology of internet domains. 

The root domain is unnamed and contains all the top-level domains. Generally, top-level domain names (TLDs) are categorized into country-code TLDs (such as .us, .ca, and .uk) and generic TLDs (such as .com, .org, .net, .edu, and .gov).

When you search for information on the web, it’s good practice to verify that the top-level domain is a trusted one. For example, when researching academic information, .edu can be a trusted TLD; for government-related searches, .gov is a good choice.

Other Elements of Credibility 

  • Author:
    • What do you know about the author and their credentials?
    • Do they have other publications?
  • Bias:
    • Can you sense any bias or specific inclinations in the writing?
    • Is the purpose of the website to inform you or persuade you?
  • Currency:
    • When was this information last updated?
    • Does the source have a date?
  • Citations:
    • Are citations or bibliography provided?
    • Are you able to check the credibility of the citations?
