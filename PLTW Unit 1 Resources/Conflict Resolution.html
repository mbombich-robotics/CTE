Conflict Resolution Strategies

Conflict can occur in a variety of situations and is a natural part of life. Conflict can be constructive or destructive. It might start with a simple disagreement but can quickly escalate to something much more, if you do not have the skills to handle the situation. 

Positive conflict-resolution strategies can help when you need to identify, approach, analyze, and then resolve conflict. To consider where you should start when faced with a conflict, review the four strategies detailed below.   

Recognize that everyone has biased perceptions of fairness, and be careful of having an “us versus them” mentality.

All people involved in a conflict have their own perceptions of what would constitute a fair resolution. It can be difficult to see a situation from another person’s point of view, but by making an attempt to overcome self-centered perceptions, you can put yourself in a better position to discuss the conflict.

biasedUnfairly prejudiced for or against someone or something. 

Try to find connections with the people you are working with to help you better understand each other's views. The more connections you can find, the more collaborative and productive your conflict-resolution process is likely to be.

Pause, breathe, and decide on next steps.

Your first step should always be to take a deep breath and think about your reaction. Immediate threats or attention-getting moves, such as take-it-or-leave-it offers, are mistakes. By taking a few minutes to pause and think rather than react, you can save yourself from making things worse. 

Then, decide your next steps. Is this part of a larger conflict? Is this something that can be resolved later? Should you move forward with trying to solve it now? Should you seek help from another source such as a teacher, mentor, or administrator?

After thinking, it’s a good idea to work with the other people involved to acknowledge that something occurred and discuss the next steps. You might encourage others to share their ideas before you move into resolving the issue. If the situation is still too sensitive, it may be helpful to wait for a more appropriate time to discuss possible solutions. 

Use good communication and active listening techniques to address the conflict.

The following communication techniques are vital in working through conflicts: 

  • Ensure everyone has a chance to talk. Allowing everyone to be heard can often clear the air right from the start—and then you can dive into the actual issue.

  • Use active listening techniques(opens in a new tab). For example: 

    • Repeat your understanding of the issues to solidify your own understanding and give the other person a chance to correct you if you’ve misinterpreted their words.

    • Allow time to process the conversation. Silence can be awkward, but it’s important to allow time for everyone to carefully consider questions or start statements that can be difficult for them.  

    • Use “I” statements to address emotions or reactions to the issue, such as starting sentences with, “I think...” or “I’ve noticed...” By speaking from your perspective, you avoid placing blame or focusing on emotions and reactions, which helps stick to the facts and solutions to an issue.

Understand when the situation is out of your hands.

Regardless of your good communication efforts and attempts to understand other points of view, some situations result in no resolution. If that’s the case, try asking for outside help. It might be as simple as bringing in another person who is unbiased in the situation. If the situation is too messy, bring it to your teacher or someone else at the school.
