Citing Sources

The purpose for citing the sources of information you use in reports and presentations you create is threefold. First, it gives credibility to the information you are presenting. If your source is a scientific journal, such as Scientific American or Nature, the information has more credibility than if the source is the National Enquirer magazine found at supermarket checkouts. Second, it enables someone to look up the source to get more information. Third, the citation gives credit to the original author(s). Just as you want credit for writing your report or your presentation, the person or persons who wrote the information you used in your report want credit for their work. 

Citation Style 

Who Determines the Format for Citations? 

The rules for citation style as published in the Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association, seventh edition, are commonly accepted for use in scientific research and writing. PLTW curriculum follows many of these style rules for citation as adapted in the examples and protocols in this document. PLTW recommends to use the APA manual as a reference in the classroom or laboratory.

Where in My Work Do I Cite My Information Sources?

You provide the sources of information in two places within your report or presentation. The first place is immediately following the information you use from the source. This is called an in-text citation because it is on the same line or in the same paragraph as the information. The second place you provide the source is in a list at the end of your work in a Bibliography or References section.

TermDefinition
in-text citationPlaced immediately after the information you use, an in-text citation briefly identifies the source of this information, which is cited in full in your reference list. It usually consists of the author’s last name and year of publication.

In-Text Citations

How Do I Write an In-Text Citation?

APA style uses the author-date citation format. With this format, readers can find the sources you cite in text in the References section, where each source is fully cited in an alphabetical list. 

  • To create a citation within text, include the author’s surname and year of publication in parentheses: (Robertson, 2019). The citation goes inside the period of the sentence or last sentence of a paraphrased paragraph.

  • For a direct quotation, include the page number or specific location of the phrase or sentences in the original work: (Poll, 2019, p. 3).

  • If there are two authors, cite both names using this format: (Walker & Allen, 2019).

  • If there are more than two authors, cite the first author’s name using this format: (Walker et al., 2019).

  • If the name of the author is unknown, use the name of the organization or the title of the website home page instead of author name: (National Research Council, 2012).

  • For a group author that has an acronym or abbreviation, use this format for the first citation: (National Institute of Mental Health [NIMH], 2020). For subsequent citations, use the abbreviation: (NIMH, 2020).

TermDefinition
et al.Latin abbreviation meaning “and others.” Used to stand in for two or more authors names.

Reference List Citations

The format for reference list citations is more detailed. However, the general format is the same for the different types of sources, including books, journals, newspapers, and web content.

How Do I Write a Reference List Citation? 

The following sections provide specific formats and examples of reference entries for the most commonly used types of sources. These examples are the ones you are most likely to use. If you use a source that does not match these examples, you can see whether your school has the Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association, ask your teacher or librarian for help, or refer to the Resources at the end of this topic. 

IMPORTANT: Examine each of the examples carefully, because the punctuation, capitalization, and italicization are very specific and different from what you normally use.

List the citations alphabetically by the first author’s last name and indent after the first line. If you use more than one resource by the same author, then list the citations for that author chronologically from earliest to most recent. 

General Reference Format

Although periodicals include a wide variety of types, their reference entries follow a general format.

Author, A. A., Author, B. B., & Author, C. C. (year). Title of the article. Title of Periodical, volume number(issue number), p–p. http://xxxxxxxxxxx

NOTE: Periodicals include items published on a regular basis such as journals, magazines, newspapers, and newsletters. Periodicals can be in print or online.

Notice that you do not capitalize the article title like it is in the work itself. Capitalize only the first letter. If the title includes a subtitle after a punctuation mark, capitalize the first letter after the mark.
