Two Phases, One Lesson

PHASE 1
CAD Assembly

Seat the MR128 bearings into C2 inside Fusion 360. This is your digital proof-of-concept — you're verifying that the parts fit together before anyone has touched a printer.

PHASE 2
Physical Build

Press the real bearings into the printed C2. Spin-test it. Measure the actual fit and compare to what you expected. If something's off, iterate — adjust C2, reprint, retest.

Both phases produce deliverable evidence — screenshots from Phase 1, a photo of the assembled spinner from Phase 2.

Why Assemble in Fusion 360?

What the assembly shows you

  • Whether the bearing sits flush in the pocket (not proud, not recessed)
  • Whether the center bearing's inner race is accessible for gripping
  • Whether the three arm bearings are evenly spaced — balance check without printing
  • A clean isometric view for your D_P1B screenshot

What it can't tell you

  • Whether the fit is tight enough — your printer's tolerance determines that, not the model
  • Whether the spinner will wobble — only a real spin test reveals that
  • How the part feels in hand — weight, balance, grip

The CAD assembly is a visual and dimensional check, not a functional test. You still have to build it.

Phase 1 — F360 Assembly Steps

  1. Open your C2_Spinner_Body design. In the Browser, confirm the C2 component is present and active.
  2. Go to Insert → Insert into Current Design. Browse to your saved C1_MR128_Bearing file. Click Open.
  3. A bearing body appears in the viewport. Use Move/Copy to position it over the center pocket. Align the bearing center to the pocket center on the Z-axis.
  4. Right-click the bearing in the Browser → Ground it in place. (Grounding fixes the component so it doesn't drift.)
  5. Insert the C1 component three more times — once for each arm pocket. Position each one at its arm pocket center.
  6. Orbit around the assembly. Confirm each bearing sits flush with the C2 face — no overhang, no gap.
  7. Take two screenshots: one isometric view (ViewCube corner), one top-down view. Save them for D_P1B.

Phase 2 — Physical Assembly

Pressing bearings in

  • Lay C2 flat on the table (smooth side up)
  • Position a bearing over a pocket — outer race down, centered
  • Press straight down with your thumb, or use a flat block to distribute the force evenly
  • Listen for a soft click — that's the bearing seating fully flush
  • Repeat for all four pockets: three arm pockets + center
  • Check flush from the side — no bearing should sit proud of the surface

If it doesn't press in cleanly

  • Too tight (won't start): use a clamp with flat pads, or gently sand the pocket opening with 220-grit. Don't force it or the body may crack.
  • Too loose (falls out): a single drop of CA glue in the pocket before pressing. Hold 30 sec. Don't glue the inner race — only the outer.
  • Tilted entry: keep pressure dead-center. A tilted press can crack thin walls.

The Spin Test

Pinch the inner race of the center bearing between thumb and index finger. Flick one of the arms. Watch what happens.
What you observe What it means Fix
Spins freely, stays level Good fit, balanced mass none — take your photo
Wobbles or tilts One or more bearings not fully seated, or arm mass is unequal press bearings fully flush; check arm symmetry in F360
Stops quickly Bearing rubbing against pocket wall, or dirty bearing check pocket size; clean bearing with isopropyl alcohol
Arm bearing falls out during spin Pocket too loose CA glue — or redesign pocket diameter
Body cracked at pocket Wall too thin or bearing forced in at an angle increase wall thickness in F360; reprint

The Iteration Loop

🔍
Measure
Calipers on the real part. Compare to your model.
→
✏️
Adjust
Change one dimension in F360. Only one at a time.
→
🖨️
Reprint
Print the full C2 or a single-arm test piece.
→
🔄
Test
Press in bearings. Spin. Did it improve?
→
✅
Done
Spins freely, bearings seated. Document and deliver.
Change one thing at a time. If you adjust the pocket size AND the arm width in the same print, you won't know which change fixed (or caused) the problem.
