What Engineers Measure

Physical Dimensions

Length, width, height, diameter, depth, thickness

Shape Features

Radius, chamfer angle, curvature, profile deviation

Position & Fit

Clearances, offsets, spacing, alignment

Mass & Weight

Total mass, center of gravity, weight distribution

Surface Quality

Roughness, flatness, texture, waviness

Material Properties

Hardness, density, tensile strength, elasticity

In this class we focus on physical dimensions and fit — measuring parts to check they match their design and fit their mating components.

Caliper — Four Measurements, One Tool

OD
Outside Jaws
Measure outer diameter or width. Close jaws on the outside of the part.
ID
Inside Jaws
Measure inner diameter or gap. Open jaws inside a hole or slot.
D
Depth Rod
Measure depth of a hole or slot. Extend the rod into the feature.
Step
Step Surface
Measure height difference between two surfaces.

Reading the Display

  • Zero the caliper before each measurement — press the zero button with jaws closed
  • Read in mm (not inches) — confirm the unit shown on display
  • Take 3 readings and average if precision matters
  • Close the caliper and verify it returns to zero

Common Mistakes

  • Measuring at an angle — jaws must be perpendicular to the surface
  • Not zeroing — stale zero gives a consistently wrong reading
  • Too much closing force — deforms soft material, gives low reading

Today's Activities

Your workstation has a 608 bearing and an M5 screw. Measure each part and record all values with units.

  1. Tool Demonstration — Instructor-led caliper walkthrough. Follow along with your own caliper.
  2. Bearing Dimensions — Measure and record the three bearing dimensions: OD (outer diameter), ID (inner diameter), and width.
  3. Screw Dimensions — Measure and record the M5 screw head width (W) and head height (H), plus the shaft diameter.
  4. Record Everything — All measurements go in your engineering notebook with units. No rounding — write exactly what the display shows.
