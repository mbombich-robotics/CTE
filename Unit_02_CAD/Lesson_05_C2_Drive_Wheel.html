Component Overview

The robot now uses a commercial AndyMark 4″ wheel. These are precision-manufactured, high-grip, and proven — better than a printed tire in almost every way. Your job is no longer to make the wheel itself. Instead, you design a hub cap that does two things:

Anti-Rotation — 6 Pins

Six pins press into the wheel's existing hole pattern. If the cap just sat flat with no pins, the motor torque could spin the cap around the wheel face. The pins transfer the torque from cap to wheel.

Retention — 10-32 Screw

A single socket-head cap screw through the center holds the cap on the wheel and holds the wheel on the motor shaft. The counterbore recesses the head so it sits flush with the cap face.

Fusion 360 Techniques

What's New This Lesson

New: Measuring Reference Parts

  • Measure tool (I) — click any edge or face in a manufacturer's model to read its exact dimension; never guess a fit-critical hole
  • Mixed-unit math in the dimension field: 0.159in - 0.25mm → Fusion converts and computes the result automatically
  • Circumscribed Polygon — hex bore profile; add H/V constraint to fix rotation; set flat-to-flat to 3/8″; switch to construction line; then Offset at −0.25 mm to get the actual extrudable hex (print clearance)
  • Circular Pattern (sketch) → Create → Circular Pattern; select the single pin circle; center = origin; quantity = 6; Fusion spaces them 60° apart

New: Three-Extrusion Sequence

  • Extrusion 1 — Disc body: select all profiles except the center hole; +2 mm outward
  • Extrusion 2 — Pins + hex insert: make sketch visible; select 6 pin circles + offset hex polygon; −2 mm (back face, toward wheel). This also creates the standoff gap.
  • Extrusion 3 — Counterbore boss: Press E → set Start = Object, click the outer disc face; hide bodies; select all profiles except the center hole and the 9.5 mm counterbore ring; +2.5 mm. Builds material around the recess so the screw head sits recessed flush.
  • Finish: Fillet all pin faces + hex insert face at 0.5 mm for easy assembly fit

Carry-Forward Rules (from C1)

  • Sketch fully constrained (all black) before extruding — no blue lines
  • File name: Lastname_HubCap_v1-Base; save new version on each print: v2-PinFit, v3-CBore
  • All fit-critical dimensions driven by parameters or explicit dimension constraints — not by dragging

Deliverable: C2 Wheel Hub Cap

Due end of Week 4 — tiered grading

Baseline
  • 6 pins match AndyMark hole pattern — correct diameter and bolt circle
  • Counterbore correctly sized for 10-32 socket head (head Ø + clearance; correct depth)
  • Cap OD fits within the wheel rim face
  • Iteration log: at least one printed version measured; record what you adjusted and why
  • Screenshots: front view (pins visible) + isometric (cap depth/face visible)
Minor Improvement
  • Baseline requirements fully met, plus one of:
  • Chamfers or fillets on pin tips (easier insertion) or cap edges
  • Embossed name, initials, or design on the cap face
  • Lightening holes or a decorative face pattern that doesn't compromise pin or counterbore geometry
  • Any aesthetic or minor ergonomic addition — no written rationale required
Substantial Improvement
  • Baseline requirements fully met, plus a functional design change with written rationale:
  • Modified pin geometry (tapered lead-in, longer engagement, press-fit vs. slip-fit reasoning)
  • Recessed boss or material reduction that maintains structural integrity — show your reasoning
  • A retention or alignment feature not in the baseline design
  • Any change must be engineering-justified — explain what you changed and why it's better
