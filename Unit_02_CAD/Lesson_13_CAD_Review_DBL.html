Why sketch in 2D first? The sketch is the blueprint. Every dimension and relationship is locked in before Fusion 360 builds the solid — so changing one number updates the whole part, not just one face.
  • A closed profile is required — open loops won't extrude
  • One sketch can drive multiple extrude operations
  • Editing the sketch later updates the 3D body automatically

Core Concept

Degrees of Freedom

  • A degree of freedom (DOF) is one way a point or line can still move
  • A single point in 2D has 2 DOF — it can slide in X and in Y
  • A line has 4 DOF — both endpoints can move independently
  • Each constraint removes one or more DOF until nothing can move
🔵

Blue

Under-constrained — some DOF still remain. The geometry can still drift.

⚫

Black

Fully constrained — every DOF is locked. The sketch is complete and repeatable.

🟡

Yellow

Over-constrained — conflicting rules applied. Remove or relax one constraint.

Goal: all geometry turns black before you extrude. A blue sketch will print differently every time someone opens the file.
Constraints Toolbar

The Key Constraints

Coincident Forces two points to share the same location — the most common constraint; snaps corners together
Horiz / Vert Forces a line to be exactly horizontal or vertical — removes one rotational DOF
Parallel Two lines run in the same direction — they never converge, even if lengths differ
Perpendicular Two lines meet at exactly 90° — guarantees square corners
Tangent A line or arc meets a curve without a corner — smooth, continuous transition
Equal Two lengths or radii share the same value — change one, both update
Midpoint A point is constrained to the center of a line — great for centering geometry
Fix / Ground Locks a point to a specific coordinate in space — anchors the sketch to the origin
CHECK — Which Constraint?

Read the scenario. Name the constraint.

1 · Your doorstop sketch has a corner where two lines meet, but they're not quite touching — there's a tiny gap, so the profile won't close.
✅ Coincident — snap the endpoints together so the gap closes and the profile is closed.
2 · The bottom of your doorstop is supposed to be flat, but when you drag it in the sketch, it tilts slightly instead of staying horizontal.
✅ Horizontal — locks the bottom edge to exactly 0° so it can't tilt.
3 · You want two circular holes in your part to have identical diameters so you only have to change one dimension to resize both.
✅ Equal — links the two radii; editing one circle updates both automatically.
4 · Your sketch drifts when someone opens the file on a different computer — the whole profile is the right shape, but it's floating in the wrong place.
✅ Fix / Ground (or a Coincident to the origin point) — anchors the sketch to a known location in the coordinate system.
The Goal

Fully Constrained Sketches

  • Fully constrained = zero remaining degrees of freedom — no point can move
  • In Fusion 360: all lines and arcs turn black when the last DOF is removed
  • A fully constrained sketch prints the same way every time — no surprises when you share the file or iterate

How to Get There

  • Add a Fix / Ground or Coincident-to-origin to anchor the sketch
  • Apply a dimension to every length and angle that isn't driven by a constraint
  • Watch the color — chase each remaining blue segment until none are left
  • Use Sketch Palette to confirm: "Fully Constrained" appears in the status bar

Common Misses

  • Sketch is the right shape but can still translate as a whole (not anchored)
  • One segment still blue — a length or angle dimension is missing
  • Two coincident constraints on the same point — over-constrained, turns yellow
  • Forgetting that arcs have radius AND position — both need constraints
Constraints are free — they don't change the look of the part. Dimensions cost a number. Use constraints first, then dimension only what's left.
Solid Tools

Extrude — Three Operations

🟩

New Body

Creates a brand-new solid. Use this for the first feature of any part — the foundation everything else builds on.

🔵

Join

Adds material to an existing body. Use for tabs, bosses, ribs — anything that grows out of the base.

🔴

Cut

Removes material from an existing body. Use for holes, slots, notches — any void inside the part.

  • You must select a closed profile first — open loops produce no solid
  • The distance field controls depth — match it to your design intent or a measured dimension
  • Fusion 360 usually guesses the right operation based on context; verify before pressing OK
CHECK — Extrude Operations

Which operation? Explain your reasoning.

5 · You've sketched the triangular side profile of your doorstop. This is the first feature in a new file.
✅ New Body — there's no existing solid yet, so this extrude creates the foundation.
6 · Your doorstop body is done. You've sketched a small bump on the top face to keep the door from sliding off the wedge when the door pushes down.
✅ Join — the bump adds material onto an existing body. Join merges it into one solid.
7 · You want to remove the toe of the doorstop (the sharp front edge) and replace it with a flat face — you've sketched a rectangle over the area to remove.
✅ Cut — you're subtracting material from the body. The rectangle's extrusion will carve out the toe.
8 · After extruding, the part looks right but feels thinner than your design. Where do you make the change?
✅ Edit the extrude feature (double-click it in the timeline) and update the distance value — not the sketch. The sketch controls the profile shape; the extrude controls the depth.
