Why Model Something You're Buying?

The MR128 bearing is a purchased part — you won't 3D print it.
So why model it in Fusion 360?
  • Your C2 spinner body needs bearing pockets that fit the bearing exactly — model it first so you design around the real dimensions.
  • It's low-stakes practice — two concentric circles and one extrude. Simple geometry, precise dimensions.
  • You end up with a reference body you can place into your assembly to check fit before printing anything.
  • In industry, engineers model every purchased component — fasteners, bearings, motors — so assemblies can be checked digitally.

Modeling the MR128 Bearing in Fusion 360

Instructor video coming soon — follow the steps on the next slide.

  • Open Fusion 360 and start a new design
  • You'll create one component, one sketch, one extrude
  • Total model time: about 10–15 minutes

Model It — Step by Step

  1. In the Browser panel, right-click Components → New Component. Name it MR128_Bearing. Activate it.
  2. Select Sketch → Create Sketch. Click the XY plane (top face).
  3. Draw a circle centered on the origin: diameter 8 mm (this is the ID — the hole through the center).
  4. Draw a second concentric circle from the same origin: diameter 12 mm (the OD).
  5. Add dimensions to both circles. Confirm the sketch is fully constrained (no blue lines).
  6. Click Finish Sketch. Select the ring profile (the area between the two circles).
  7. Extrude the ring profile 3.5 mm in the positive Z direction.
  8. Apply an Appearance: search Steel – Satin. Drag onto the body.
  9. Save the file as MR128_Bearing_C1.

The Fully-Constrained Check

Before finishing the sketch, confirm all four conditions below.

⚫

All lines are black

Blue geometry = still free to move. Black = locked in place. You want no blue.

🎯

Center fixed to origin

Both circles must be centered on the origin point. Use a Coincident constraint if needed.

📐

Both diameters dimensioned

⌀8 mm and ⌀12 mm both shown. No driven dimensions (no grey numbers).

✅

Bottom-left of the sketch workspace shows the constraint count and status.

Press-fit pocket

  • Pocket diameter = 12.0 mm (exact match)
  • Bearing must be pressed in — won't come out by hand
  • Relies on your printer's tolerance being tight
  • Good for permanent installation

Slip-fit pocket

  • Pocket diameter = 12.1–12.2 mm
  • Bearing drops in and can be removed
  • More forgiving of printer variation
  • May need a drop of adhesive to hold

You'll decide which fit to use in Lesson 2.5. For now, model the bearing at exactly 12 mm OD.

Lab: Measure Before You Trust

Get a real MR128 bearing from the supply bin. Measure it with calipers. Record your results.
