The Problem it Solves

When One Part Isn't Enough

One-Part Limits

  • Object must exit cleanly from the open top
  • Undercuts — any feature that would lock the mold — are a problem
  • Only one face of the object is defined by the mold
  • The bottom (where it rests in the mold) ends up flat

Two-Part Solves It

  • Each half defines one side of the object — both halves together define the full shape
  • Handles complex geometry, undercuts, enclosed cavities
  • Used for everything from rubber ducks to engine gaskets
  • Requires more design work — alignment must be precise
Key vocabulary: Parting line — where the two halves split. Registration keys — bumps and recesses that lock the halves in alignment. Pour spout — channel that lets material in. Vents — holes that let trapped air out.

Key Concepts from the Video

  • Parting line choice: pick the line where the geometry has the least undercut on each side — usually the object's widest cross-section
  • First pour: silicone poured on one half of the positive while it sits in a clay or foam bed; second pour goes on top after first half cures
  • Mold release: apply release agent to the first silicone half before the second pour — otherwise they bond into a solid block
  • Registration bumps (keys) are often formed naturally by the silicone flowing around the first half's edges, or added intentionally with clay divots
  • Pour spout location should be at the highest point of the cavity when the mold is in pour position — air rises, material sinks
P4 — A-Level Path

Rigid Two-Part PETG Tooling

A-Level — P4 Instead of a silicone mold, you'll design and print two PETG mold halves in Fusion 360 — one for each side of your wax candle shape. PETG can handle wax pour temperatures (55–65°C) without deforming. You bolt or clamp the halves, pour wax in through the spout, let it cool, and open.
  • Design both halves in Fusion 360: model the full candle shape, then use a split body to create the two mold halves
  • Add alignment pins: small cylindrical bosses on one half mate with holes in the other — keep the seam tight
  • Pour spout + vent: spout goes in, trapped air must have somewhere to go out
  • Parting line = seam line on your finished candle — choose it where it's least visible or easiest to trim
