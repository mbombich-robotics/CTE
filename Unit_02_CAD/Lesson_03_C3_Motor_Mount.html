Component Overview

C3 — Motor Sleeve Mount

How it works

The motor's outer flange is removed. The motor body slides into the sleeve bore. The flange is reattached through the front face of the mount using four 10-32 × ½″ screws. The T-slot rail on top allows M5 nuts to slide in from the end before bolting the mount to the deck from above — no under-deck access needed during assembly.

Key concept: T-slot geometry

The slot cavity must be wide enough for the nut to slide in, and the top opening must be narrow enough to trap the nut while still letting the bolt shaft through. Getting either dimension wrong by even 0.3 mm breaks the mechanism.

F360 Techniques

What's new this lesson

Inspect → Measure

Click two faces or edges on the provided motor model to get exact dimensions. Use these values directly in your sleeve bore sketch and screw hole pattern — no guessing, no calipers.

Channel profile (T-slot)

Sketch the T cross-section on the end face of the mount top. The T has a narrow top slot and a wider bottom cavity. Extrude-cut the full length of the rail so the nut can enter from either end.

Hole tool (face screws)

4 holes on the front face matching the motor flange bolt circle. Use Inspect → Measure to get the bolt circle diameter and hole size from the motor model before placing holes.

Sketch on a non-XZ plane

The T-slot cross-section sketch goes on the end face of the rail — not the default XZ plane. Select the correct face before activating Sketch or your geometry will land in the wrong location.

Think through assembly before you model

Work through the assembly sequence mentally first:

  1. Nut slides into T-slot from the end
  2. Mount sits on the deck
  3. M5 bolt comes down through deck into captured nut

If the slot doesn't run full length, the nut can't get in. If the top opening is too wide, the nut falls through. Test this mentally before you print.
