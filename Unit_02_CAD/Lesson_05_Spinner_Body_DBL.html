The pockets are not.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1

Apply a circular pattern to place three bearing pockets at 120° spacing

2

Size bearing pockets to accept the MR128 (12mm OD) from your C1 model

3

Design a flat body that prints support-free face-down on the build plate

What C2 Has to Do

  • 🔵 Four bearing pockets — one center, one per arm All sized to your C1 OD. The pockets are the one dimension you don't get to freestyle.
  • 🤌 Center pocket is the handle — pinch the inner race to hold the spinner The center bearing's outer race is pressed into C2. You hold the inner race still. The spinner body rotates around it.
  • ⚖️ Balanced mass distribution — circular pattern ensures it Three arms at 120° each. If one arm is heavier, the spinner wobbles.
  • 📏 Body thickness = bearing width — 3.5mm Level 1 approach: the body is exactly 3.5mm so bearing pockets go all the way through. Bearings press in from one side.
  • 🖨️ Printable without supports — keep it flat A flat 3.5mm body prints face-down with no overhangs. Don't add features that face downward.

Your Design Choice — The Arm Shape

The arm profile connecting the center to each bearing pocket is yours to design. There is no wrong shape — as long as the pocket circles are correctly placed and sized.
Oval Arm
Simple. Easy to constrain. Good starting point.
Rectangle
Fast to sketch. Add fillets to round the corners.
Teardrop
Tapers toward center. Looks professional, harder to sketch.
Diamond
Sharp angles. Use Fillet to take the edge off.

Whatever arm shape you choose, the pocket circles never change — they're sized to the bearing OD.

The Plan — Think Pocket-First

Where the pockets live

  • Center pocket — circle at the origin. Your center bearing goes here.
  • Arm pockets — one at the end of each arm, all the same distance from the origin.
  • Common arm length (origin to arm-pocket center): 25–35mm. Shorter = compact, longer = more spin stability.
  • All pocket circles are the same diameter — your target fit size from Lesson 2.2.

Sketch order

  • Draw the center pocket circle at the origin first — this anchors everything.
  • Place the first arm pocket circle on the Y-axis (straight up from origin).
  • Draw your arm profile connecting center to arm pocket.
  • Circular Pattern the arm + arm pocket × 3 at 360°.
Key rule: Place the arm pocket circle center on a construction line from the origin. Dimension that line (your arm length) — this is the one dimension that lets you quickly resize the whole spinner.

Steps — Part 1: Build the Sketch

  1. New Component → name it C2_Spinner_Body. Make sure it's active (bold in the Browser).
  2. Create Sketch → XY plane.
  3. Draw a circle at the origin: your target pocket diameter (e.g. ⌀22.1mm). This is the center bearing pocket.
  4. Draw a construction line from the origin straight up (along Y-axis). Dimension it to your arm length (e.g. 30mm).
  5. At the top of that construction line, draw another circle — same pocket diameter. This is the first arm bearing pocket.
  6. Draw your arm profile shape connecting the edge of the center pocket to the edge of the arm pocket. Ensure the profile is fully closed — no gaps.
  7. Circular Pattern (Sketch menu): select the arm pocket circle + arm profile lines. Pattern type: Circular. Quantity: 3. Full angle: 360°. Click OK.
  8. Fully dimension the sketch (arm shape + all pocket circles). Verify no blue lines remain.

Steps — Part 2: Extrude & Bore

  1. Finish Sketch. The sketch should show the body profile with the pocket circles as holes in it.
  2. Select the solid regions of the body (the arm and center frame — everything except the pocket circles). Do not select the pocket interiors.
  3. Extrude → 3.5mm (positive Z direction). The pocket holes go all the way through automatically.
  4. Inspect your body: you should see a flat 3.5mm spinner with four through-holes — one at center, one per arm. The center bearing's 8mm inner race is naturally exposed through the center pocket.
  5. Apply an Appearance if desired (Plastic — Matte works well for a printed part).
  6. Save as C2_Spinner_Body. Take a screenshot of the isometric view for your records.

Pocket Sizing — Choose Your Fit

All four bearing pockets use the same diameter. The MR128 OD is 12.0mm. Your pocket diameter sets the fit.

12.0 mm
Press-Fit

Pocket matches bearing OD exactly. Bearing must be pressed in — won't come out by hand. Relies on your printer being accurate. Start here for Level 1.

12.1–12.2 mm
Slip-Fit + Glue

Pocket is slightly larger — bearing drops in easily. Use a small drop of CA glue to hold it. More forgiving if your printer runs large.

Tip: Start with 12.0mm. Print one arm as a test piece first (just one lobe, not the full body). Check the fit. Adjust the pocket dimension, then print the full C2.

Before You Print — Check These

Sketch problems

  • Profile won't select? There's a gap in your arm outline. Zoom all the way in to each endpoint — look for a tiny break.
  • Blue lines after pattern? Add dimensions or constraints to the patterned geometry. Circular Pattern doesn't auto-constrain everything.
  • Arm overlaps center pocket? Your arm length is too short. Increase it or shrink the arm width near the center.

Design checks

  • Are all three arms identical? Inspect the circular pattern — it should show 3 equal instances.
  • Is the center pocket a clean through-hole? The center bearing's inner race (8mm) should be visible and accessible when the bearing is seated.
  • Is your body exactly 3.5mm? Check the extrude distance in the Timeline. This must match your bearing width.
  • No supports needed? Flat bodies print support-free. If BambuStudio adds supports, your orientation is wrong.
