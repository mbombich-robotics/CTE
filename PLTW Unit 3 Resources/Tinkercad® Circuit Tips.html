Tinkercad® Circuit Tips

Saving Your Work

Autodesk® Tinkercad® will automatically save your work. Be sure to rename any new circuits you create to avoid overwriting existing ones (shown in the How to Rename Your Circuit video). 

How to Rename Your Circuit video (The video has no sound.) 

IMPORTANT:  Do not add more than one circuit to your design. After two weeks, Tinkercad removes all except one circuit within a given file. 

Running the Simulator

After completing a circuit, select Start Simulation to check your work and see what happens.

  • Download an image of the circuit. 
  • Invite people to view and edit your project using a link.

Connecting Components in a Circuit

To add wires to your Tinkercad® circuit, select a terminal of the battery, and then select the next terminal you want to connect it to.

  • To see whether a terminal is positive or negative, hover over the terminal.

  • To create a bend in the connecting wire before you connect it to a terminal, click in the open space where you want the bend to be.

Rotating Components in a Circuit

To rotate an object, select it, then press R on your keyboard. The object rotates 45 degrees with each press of the R key.

Connecting a Slide Switch

When you connect a slide switch, always connect a wire to the Common terminal in the center and a wire to either Terminal 1 or Terminal 2 (Figure 1).

Note that when you start the simulation, the default state of a slide switch is ON.

NOTE: When you use a slide switch as a digital input controlled by a microcontroller, connect one terminal to positive power, the other terminal toward the ground, and the Common terminal to a digital input pin on the microcontroller.

Connecting a Push Button

When you use a pushbutton switch in Tinkercad®, connect a wire to a terminal on the “1” side (“1a” or “1b”) and a wire to a terminal on the “2” side (“2a” or “2b”) as shown in Figure 2. 

Connecting LEDs 

LEDs allow current to flow in only one direction. Current flows from the positive leg of the LED to the negative leg of the LED and never in the opposite direction. The positive leg of the LED is called the “anode” and is identified by its longer “lead.” The negative leg of the LED is called the “cathode.”

Connecting a Potentiometer

A potentiometer has three legs: two outside terminals and a wiper in the middle. 

TermDefinition
wiperA terminal which moves over a resistive section of a device, changing the resistance on either side of it.   

You can wire a potentiometer one of two ways, depending on how you want to use it. 

  • Variable Resistor
  • Voltage Divider

Variable Resistor

When you want the potentiometer to act as a variable resistor, connect one wire from your circuit to the wiper and the second wire to terminal 1 (the left-most terminal in Figure 4). One leg serves as the input and the other as the output.

NOTE: If you connect the second wire to terminal 2 (the right-most terminal in Figure 5) instead of terminal 1, it will still work, but the potentiometer’s lowest and highest resistance settings will be flipped on the dial.

Voltage Divider

Connect all three legs as follows: wires from the terminals to 5 V and ground (0), and a wire from the wiper that outputs a variable voltage (anywhere from 0 to 5 V) to another component in the circuit (Figure 6).

NOTE: Using a potentiometer as a voltage divider shifts its function from altering resistance to altering voltage. 

Changing the Settings of Components

It is possible to change the settings of some components in Tinkercad®, including the following: 

  • Number of 1.5 V batteries used. 

    • Select the component, then change the battery setting in the Count box as shown in Figure 7.

NOTE: Tinkercad allows the user to select one, two, three, or four batteries in the Count box menu.

  • Resistance value of a resistor. 
    • Select the resistor, then change the resistance value in the Resistance box as shown in Figure 8.
  • Value that a multimeter measures. 
    • To change the value, while the simulation is running, click the A, V, or R button on the multimeter (Figure 9). 

NOTE: Some adjustments can be made only while the simulation is running; other adjustments can be made only when the simulation is stopped. If you do not see the options you need, try starting or stopping the simulation.
