Your Component Design Project

This is the project you'll be designing for. Not a practice problem — the actual thing you'll 3D print and use on your robot (or take home spinning).

HS & 8th Grade AER

C1 — Wheel Hub

  • Supports the omni wheel on a fixed M8 bolt axle
  • Houses an MR128 bearing (12mm OD × 8mm ID × 3.5mm) — one per hub half
  • PLA, printed in class, iterated if needed
  • Both halves work together — similar but not identical

Design & Build Lab

C1 — Fidget Spinner

  • Spins on a 608 bearing (22mm OD × 7mm deep)
  • Symmetric geometry for balanced rotation
  • PLA, printed in class — arm design is yours
  • You design it; it must seat the bearing and spin freely

Before you model anything in CAD, you write a design brief. That's what this lesson teaches you to do.

What Is a Design Brief? PLTW

The document that defines the project before building starts. It's the contract between your team and the problem.

1. Problem Statement

Who needs what and why

2. Criteria

What the solution should do (measurable goals)

3. Constraints

What the solution must work within (non-negotiable limits)

4. Design Statement

One sentence combining criteria + constraints

5. Concept Sketches

3 different designs before committing to one

Why professionals use them

  • Keeps everyone on the same goal
  • Prevents scope creep mid-build
  • Creates a record of every decision
  • Lets you evaluate your outcome objectively

Criteria — What It Should Do

Criteria are the goals. Measurable, rankable, and specific.

Wheel Hub Criteria

  • Hub contains MR128 bearing — no axial play; shoulder retains from each side
  • M8 bolt passes freely through the bearing bore — no binding
  • Both outer faces flush — M3 heads counterbored on entry; tips stop short of far face
  • Hub total width is student-calculated from geometry; mount built to fit with 0.5 mm clearance each side
  • Left and right halves mate — M3 self-tap pilot holes align and hold without nuts

Fidget Spinner Criteria

  • Center pocket seats 608 bearing — flush, no wobble
  • Arms are symmetric — balanced rotation, no vibration
  • Arm geometry is student-designed (3, 4, or 5 arms)
  • Prints in one session — no more than 1 hr print time
  • Spins freely and smoothly when flicked

Your team can add criteria, clarify them, or rank them by priority. That's part of the brief.

You'll get more specific criteria from the client interview in Lesson 1.6. For now, start with these.

Constraints — What You Must Work Within

Constraints are the limits. Non-negotiable — they don't change regardless of design.

Component Design Constraints (both tracks)

  • Software: Fusion 360 only — parametric CAD, not freehand
  • Material: PLA filament only — printed on the school's Bambu printers
  • Print time: must complete in one class session — design accordingly
  • Hardware is fixed — bearing and bolt sizes are not negotiable; no larger selection is feasible
  • AER: M8 bolt = 45 mm — total bolt path (mount walls + hub assembly + nut) cannot exceed this
  • Arm/spoke/outer geometry is your design choice, but must be structurally sound

Remember the shortcut:

Criteria = Goals Constraints = Limits

Later components (C2, C3…) will have different constraints, but the same mental model. Criteria = what success looks like. Constraints = what you can't change.

The Design Statement

One sentence that packages the problem statement, criteria, and constraints together.

Template

Design a [solution] that [criteria 1], [criteria 2], and [criteria 3],
using only [constraint 1], within [constraint 2].

Too vague

"Design a wheel hub for the robot."

No measurable criteria. No way to know if you succeeded.

Strong

"Design a PLA wheel hub that contains an MR128 bearing (12mm OD × 3.5mm) with no axial play, allows an M8 bolt to pass freely through the bearing bore, and mates with the opposite-side hub to support the omni wheel — modeled in Fusion 360 and printed within one class session."

You'll write yours with your team on the next slide.
