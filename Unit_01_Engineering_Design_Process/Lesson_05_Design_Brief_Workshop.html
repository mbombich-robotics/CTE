Section 1 — Client & End User

Client: the person who commissioned the work. End user: the person who will actually use the result. They are often different people — knowing both shapes what you design.

✗ Weak

"My client is students who need a better robot."

  • Which students? No one specific is named
  • Client and end user are collapsed into one vague group
  • "Better robot" is a solution, not a client

✓ Strong

"Client: Mr. B (project manager and technical lead). End users: the competition drive team — the three students who operate the robot on the field."

  • Client is a named, specific person with a role
  • End user is a distinct, specific group
  • No solution language anywhere

Section 2 — Problem Statement

The problem statement describes what is wrong, missing, or not working — not what you're going to build. One or two focused sentences. No solution language.

✗ Weak

"I will design a hub cap for the robot wheel that covers the hardware and looks clean."

  • "I will design" — that's a solution, not a problem
  • Skips over what's actually broken or missing
  • "Looks clean" is subjective and untestable

✓ Strong

"The wheel hub hardware is exposed after assembly, creating a snagging hazard on field elements and leaving the robot with an unfinished appearance during inspection."

  • States exactly what's wrong — exposed hardware
  • Two real consequences — snagging, appearance
  • No mention of a solution

Test: could someone read your problem statement and design a completely different solution? If yes, it's a problem statement. If no, it's a solution statement.

Prepare for Tomorrow

Tomorrow you interview Mr. B to discover your criteria and constraints — the information you need to fill Sections 3 & 4. Copy these questions into your engineering notebook and write the answers next to each one during the interview. Add any questions of your own at the bottom.

  • Q1What does this component need to do? Describe the function in one or two sentences.
  • Q2What does it physically connect to, and what are the dimensions at each interface?
  • Q3What are the critical dimensions — the ones that absolutely must be correct for the part to work?
  • Q4Are there any geometry requirements? (No overhangs that need supports, must be symmetric, specific wall thickness…)
  • Q5How will we test whether my design is successful? What do I measure or check?
  • Q6What constraints am I working within? (Material, print time, iteration budget…)
  • Q7Is there anything I should not do, or a common mistake to avoid?

Your notebook = your notes during the interview. No notebook tomorrow = no answers.
