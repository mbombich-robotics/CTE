Concept Sketching

Your sketch is your Concept A — the idea you're contributing to your group. One sketch, your hand, your engineering notebook. These rules are not optional.

1Pencil only. Mistakes get erased, not crossed out.
2Label everything. Every feature gets a name or a description.
3Dimension everything critical. Numbers, not "about this big."
4At least two views. Front and side (or top). One view hides information.
5One idea per sketch. This is Concept A — not A, B, and C combined.
6No CAD, no ruler required. Proportional and readable beats perfect.

See the example on the next slide — that's the standard. Your hub detail should look like the hub detail shown there.

What a Good Sketch Looks Like

Teacher example — front cross section, side view, and hub detail with labeled dimensions

Sketch Time — Concept A

15 minutes

  • Your idea, your hand, your notebook
  • Apply all six rules — label, dimension, two views
  • Your sketch becomes Concept A in your decision matrix

When time is up: photograph your sketch and email it to both group partners.

Group Exchange

You now have three concepts — yours plus two from your group. You score all three against your own criteria and weights. Your group members score with their own too — matrices may differ.

Getting the photos

  • Photo booth at the back of the room, or use your Chromebook camera
  • Email your sketch to both group partners
  • Confirm you have received both of theirs

Setting up your matrix

  • Your sketch = Concept A (first column)
  • Your partners' sketches = Concepts B and C
  • Label each column with a brief description of the concept
  • Your criteria and weights from Thursday are already in — just add the concept names across the top

You are not graded on the quality of Concepts B and C — only Concept A (yours) is evaluated for sketch quality. You are graded on whether your matrix is filled in correctly and completely.

Concept C wins at 45. Strong on the two weight-3 criteria is what matters most. If the matrix recommends something you don't want to pick — explain which criterion the matrix underweighted and why. That argument is worth writing.

Score Your Matrix

Your criteria and weights are already in from Thursday. Now fill in the scores.

Score each concept 1–5

  • 5 — fully meets this criterion
  • 3 — partially meets it
  • 1 — does not meet it

Multiply, then sum

Score × Weight = cell value. Sum each concept's column. Highest total = recommended choice. Write 2–3 sentences explaining which concept you're going with and why.
