What Is a Client Interview?

A client interview is a structured conversation between an engineer and the person who needs the solution built. Its purpose is to collect requirements that are specific, measurable, and testable — not just vague wishes.

What engineers ask about

  • Function — what must the part do?
  • Interfaces — what does it connect to?
  • Dimensions — what specific sizes matter?
  • Constraints — material, time, cost, method?
  • Success criteria — how will we know it works?

Active listening rules

  • Take notes on paper — you cannot remember this later
  • Ask clarifying questions if anything is vague
  • Never assume you know the answer before you ask
  • Repeat back what you heard: "So you're saying…"

Who is your client today?

Mr. B. He designed the component you're going to build and knows exactly what it needs to do and how it needs to fit. He is the authority on the requirements.

The Interview

Ask these questions. Write the answers on your Design Brief template.

  • Q1 What does this component need to do? Describe the function in one or two sentences.
  • Q2 What does it physically connect to, and what are the dimensions at each interface?
  • Q3 What are the critical dimensions — the ones that absolutely must be correct for the part to work?
  • Q4 Are there any geometry requirements? (For example: no overhangs that need supports, must be symmetric, specific wall thickness…)
  • Q5 How will we test whether my design is successful? What do I measure or check?
  • Q6 What constraints am I working within? (Material, print time, iteration budget…)
  • Q7 Is there anything I should not do, or a common mistake to avoid?

From Notes to Problem Statement

Compress your interview notes into one focused sentence

A problem statement explains who needs what and why. It does not describe the solution — it describes the gap.

Problem statement formula

[Who] needs a way to [do what] because [reason / current problem].

Examples by class

AER: The robot drive system needs a way to support the omni wheel on a fixed M8 axle bolt because no off-the-shelf hub fits this wheel and bearing combination.

DBL: A fidget spinner user needs a body that spins freely around a standard 608 bearing because existing designs either fail to press-fit correctly or are not balanced for smooth spin.

Your problem statement does not mention PLA, dimensions, or Fusion 360. Those are part of the solution — they do not belong in the problem.

The Design Statement

One sentence that binds criteria and constraints together

The design statement is the summary line of your design brief. It names what your solution will do and under what constraints it will do it.

Design a [component name] that [primary function], fits within [key dimension], and can be [method / material constraint].

Criteria (what it must do)

  • Come from your interview answers — not guessed
  • Must be measurable ("fits a 3mm shaft" not "fits the shaft")
  • At least three criteria

Constraints (what limits the design)

  • Material: PLA filament only
  • Method: FDM 3D printing — no overhangs > 45°
  • Iterations: two prints per student
  • Timeline: complete model by end of tomorrow
