Build the Tallest Tower

Lesson 1.2 — No framework. No process. Just build.

Lesson 1.2

Lesson Objectives

  • Build a free-standing tower using limited materials within a time limit
  • Describe what design choices worked and what caused your structure to fail
  • Explain why a structured design process leads to better results than guessing

The Challenge

Build the tallest freestanding tower you can.
The marshmallow must be at the top.

⏱️

18 minutes

👥

Pairs

📏

Tallest wins

Your Materials

Each pair receives exactly this — nothing else.

20
sticks of dry spaghetti
1 yd
masking tape
1 yd
string
1
marshmallow — must be whole and at the top of the finished tower

No other materials.

You may break the spaghetti. You may cut the tape/string. You don't get new spaghetti.

Rules

  • Tower must be freestanding — no hands supporting it at the buzzer
  • Marshmallow must be at the top and whole
  • Stay within your designated table space
  • Measurement happens at exactly 18:00 — whatever is standing at the buzzer counts
  • If your tower falls at 00:01, your height is zero
  • Measuring to the bottom of the marshmallow

Any questions before the timer starts?

GO

Open the materials. Start building.

18:00

click to reset

Debrief

Discussion Questions

  • What was your process? Did you have one?
  • What was your first move — and why?
  • When something failed, what did you do?
  • If you had 18 more minutes, what would you change?

Look at the Winner

  • What did they do differently?
  • Was it luck, or a better approach?
  • Could you copy their strategy and beat them?

Research shows business school students consistently do worse on this challenge than kindergarteners.
Kids prototype immediately. Adults plan too long.

What If You Had a Method?

What if — before you touched the spaghetti — you knew what failure modes to expect?

What if you had a structured process for defining the problem, generating options, and testing your design?

Tomorrow we learn that method.
Then we build gliders and find out.
