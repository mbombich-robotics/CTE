🔩 DRILL SAFETY

Drill Press · Cordless Drill/Driver

⚠️ BEFORE YOU TOUCH A DRILL

Drills are the most-used power tools in the shop, and that makes it easy to get careless with them. You need Mr. Bombich's training and sign-off before you use either drill on your own.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the main drilling hazards: entanglement, spinning work, flying chips, and hot bits
  • Choose the right PPE and dress for drilling, and explain why gloves are not allowed
  • Follow the posted Safe Operating Procedure checklists before, during, and after drilling
  • Know when and how to stop: release, step back, and switch off

⚡ HOW DRILLS HURT PEOPLE

🌀

Entanglement

Hair, gloves, sleeves, and hoodie strings wrap around a spinning chuck in a fraction of a second, and the drill doesn't stop.

🔄

Spinning Work

When the bit grabs an unclamped part, the part spins with it and its sharp edges become a blade. This happens most often as the bit breaks through the bottom.

🎯

Flying Chips & Keys

Chips and broken bits fly toward your eyes. A chuck key left in the chuck gets thrown out when the motor starts.

🔥

Heat & Sharp Edges

Bits and metal chips get hot enough to burn. Freshly drilled holes leave sharp burrs that need to be deburred.

🥽 PPE & DRESS CODE

✅ Safety Glasses

Always, for both drills. Wear them from the moment you pick up the tool until clean-up is finished.

✅ Closed-Toe Shoes

Hot chips, dropped parts, and dropped drills all end up on the floor.

✅ Hair Tied Back

Tie back long hair and roll up loose sleeves. Anything that hangs down can reach the chuck.

❌ No Gloves · No Jewelry

A spinning bit grabs fabric and pulls your hand in with it. Rings, watches, lanyards, and hoodie strings do the same.

Wait — no gloves? Gloves protect you from sharp edges and hot parts when the tool is off. Near a rotating tool, a glove becomes the thing that pulls you in. Take them off before you drill, and put them back on to handle hot or sharp parts afterward.

🏭 DRILL PRESS: THE POSTED SOP

This checklist is posted at the drill press. Run through it every time you use the machine.

1 · Before

  • Hole marked and center-punched
  • Pulley cover closed, no lock or tag
  • Bit tight, chuck key REMOVED
  • Work clamped, never hand-held
  • Depth stop set
  • Know where ON/OFF and the E-stop are

2 · During

  • Bit runs true, with no wobble
  • Hands 3 inches or more from the bit
  • Steady pressure and peck drilling
  • Ease off at breakthrough
  • Work spins? Let go, step back, switch OFF

3 · After

  • OFF, and let the spindle stop on its own
  • Deburr the part
  • Bit, key, and vise put away
  • Brush chips, never your hands
  • Leave it safe for the next person

🔋 CORDLESS DRILL: THE POSTED SOP

A cordless drill is smaller, but it still twists hard enough to sprain a wrist or spin a part out of your hand.

1 · Before

  • Trigger locked before touching the chuck
  • Bit seated fully and tightened
  • Direction, speed, and clutch set
  • Work clamped, never in your hand or on your lap
  • Check what's behind and under the work

2 · During

  • Firm grip, both hands
  • Free hand never behind the work
  • Binds? Release the trigger
  • Forward all the way through and out
  • Reverse only to back out a jammed bit

3 · After

  • Trigger locked, battery out
  • Bit removed (it may be hot!)
  • Battery on the charger
  • Drill put away, bench brushed clean

🚫 THE NON-NEGOTIABLES

Break one of these and you're done for the day

  • The chuck key never stays in the chuck. Put it back in its holder before power goes on.
  • The work is clamped. Never hold it in your hand, and never hold it on your lap.
  • Never grab spinning work. Let go, step back, and switch off.
  • No gloves, no jewelry, hair tied back anywhere near a spinning bit.
  • Never slow the chuck with your hand, and never clear chips while it's moving.

Something feel wrong? Stop and tell Mr. Bombich. Strange noises, wobbling bits, smoke, and a part that won't stay put are all good reasons to stop. You will never get in trouble for stopping a tool to ask.

🎯 QUICK CHECK

1. Why are gloves not allowed at the drill press?

  • They make it harder to feel how tight the chuck is.
  • A spinning bit can grab the fabric and pull your hand in.
  • They leave fibers in the hole that dull the bit faster.
  • They make it harder to read the scale on the depth stop.

2. The part you're drilling starts spinning with the bit. What do you do?

  • Grab the part firmly and pull it away from the bit.
  • Push down harder on the feed handle to finish the hole.
  • Let go of the part, step back, and switch the machine off.
  • Flip the spindle into reverse so the part unwinds itself.

3. What must happen right after you tighten a bit in the drill press chuck?

  • Remove the chuck key and put it back in its holder.
  • Run the press at top speed to seat the bit in place.
  • Loosen the chuck a quarter turn so the bit can center.
  • Oil the bit so it cuts cleanly through the material.

🎯 QUICK CHECK (PART 2)

4. When should you use reverse on the cordless drill?

  • To pull the bit back out after every hole you drill.
  • To back out a bit that has jammed in the hole.
  • To start a hole so the bit doesn't wander off the mark.
  • To drill faster through hard material like aluminum.

5. Before changing a bit on the cordless drill, you should…

  • Set the clutch ring to its highest number setting.
  • Lock the trigger by centering the direction switch.
  • Run it in reverse so the chuck loosens on its own.
  • Switch the speed selector from gear 2 down to gear 1.

6. How do you clear chips off the drill press table?

  • Wipe them off with your hand once the bit slows down.
  • Blow them off the table with the compressed air gun.
  • Use a brush after the spindle has completely stopped.
  • Sweep them away with a rag while the bit is turning.
