Fran's Hay Lifting Problem

Fran needs to lift 45 kg hay bales (F_R = 45 × 9.81 = 441 N ≈ 99 lb) into a barn loft. No machine — she lifts straight up with 441 N of force. With pulleys, we can do much better.
  • A pulley is a wheel with a grooved rim — rope or cable sits in the groove
  • Pulleys are classified as simple machines (wheel & axle variant)
  • Three types: fixed, movable, compound (block and tackle)
  • The IMA of a pulley system = number of rope segments supporting the load
  • Pulleys reduce the effort force needed — at the cost of pulling more rope
  • Energy is conserved: F_E × d_pulled = F_R × d_lifted (assuming 100% efficiency)

Fixed Pulley — Direction Changer

What It Does

  • The axle is fixed to the ceiling/support — the wheel spins but doesn't move vertically
  • You pull DOWN on one end of the rope; the load rises on the other end
  • IMA = 1 — no force multiplication; effort force = resistance force
  • Only ONE rope segment supports the load
  • Benefit: pulls DOWN instead of UP — more convenient, can use body weight

Example

  • Flagpole — you pull down to raise the flag
  • Theater curtain — pull one side of the rope to raise the curtain
  • Any simple redirecting application where the direction of force matters more than force magnitude
A fixed pulley does NOT reduce the force you need. It only changes the direction. If you need to lift 441 N, you still pull with 441 N — just downward instead of upward.

Movable Pulley — Force Multiplier

What It Does

  • The axle moves WITH the load — the pulley wheel travels up as the load rises
  • Two rope segments support the load — one side attached to a fixed point, one pulled by you
  • IMA = 2 — effort force = resistance force / 2
  • Fran's example: lift 441 N with only 220 N effort!
  • Tradeoff: you must pull 2 feet of rope to lift the load 1 foot

Why IMA = 2

  • Draw a free body diagram of the movable pulley: two upward rope tensions (T each) support the weight F_R
  • Equilibrium: 2T = F_R → T = F_R / 2 = effort force
  • Two segments supporting the load → IMA = 2
  • More segments → higher IMA

Compound Pulley — Block and Tackle

IMA of a compound pulley system = the number of rope segments supporting (attached to) the movable pulley/load. Count the rope segments — don't count the one you're pulling.
  1. Combine fixed and movable pulleys into a multi-pulley system
  2. Count rope segments touching the lower (movable) block — that number = IMA
  3. With 4 supporting segments: IMA = 4 → lift 441 N with only 110 N effort
  4. With 6 supporting segments: IMA = 6 → lift 441 N with only 73.5 N effort
  5. Tradeoff: IMA = 6 means you pull 6 feet of rope for every 1 foot the load rises
  6. Verify conservation: F_E × d_rope = F_R × d_load → 73.5 N × 6 ft = 441 N × 1 ft ✓ (ideal)

The Tradeoff — Power Is Conserved

A pulley system multiplies force but never creates energy. Whatever work you put in equals the work done on the load (minus friction losses).
  • Mathematical expression: F_E × d_E = F_R × d_R (ideal, no friction)
  • IMA = 4 → pull 4 feet to lift 1 foot AND use 1/4 the force
  • IMA = 8 → pull 8 feet to lift 1 foot AND use 1/8 the force
  • The total work is the same regardless of IMA: only force and distance change, not their product
  • Friction in the pulleys means real systems need slightly MORE effort than IMA predicts — AMA < IMA
  • AMA / IMA = efficiency of the pulley system

Applying Work and Power to Pulleys (POE_114)

Problem 1 — Power

  • Fran lifts 300 lb (1334 N) of hay 15 feet (4.57 m) in 10 minutes (600 s)
  • W = F × d = 1334 × 4.57 = 6,097 J
  • P = W / t = 6097 / 600 = 10.2 Watts
  • How much power is that in horsepower? (1 hp = 746 W → 10.2/746 = 0.014 hp — barely anything!)

Problem 2 — Time

  • A horse can deliver 100 Watts of sustained power
  • How long does it take the horse to do the same job?
  • t = W / P = 6097 J / 100 W = 61 seconds
  • vs. Fran's 600 seconds alone — the horse is 10× faster (or equivalently, 10× more powerful)
1 horsepower = 746W was defined by James Watt to make steam engines sound impressive by comparison to horses. A horse actually delivers ~750W peak but only ~100W sustained.
