What Gears Do

Gears are toothed wheels that mesh together to transfer rotation from one shaft to another. They can change speed, torque, and direction of rotation.
  • A vintage tractor engine spins at ~700 RPM. Tractor tires rotate at ~10 RPM. Gears bridge that gap.
  • Gear ratio determines how speed and torque change between input and output
  • Key insight: gears conserve power (P = τ × ω). If torque goes up, speed must go down — and vice versa.
  • Meshed teeth ensure no slip — every tooth on the driver mates with a tooth on the driven gear. Positive drive, unlike belt drives that can slip.
  • Gears also change rotation direction: two meshed gears rotate opposite to each other.

The Gear Ratio

Gear Ratio = Teeth_driven / Teeth_driver (equivalently: Diameter_driven / Diameter_driver)

Gear Ratio > 1 (Speed Reduction)

  • Driven gear has MORE teeth than driver
  • Driven gear rotates SLOWER
  • But torque INCREASES by the same factor
  • Example: 12-tooth driver → 36-tooth driven → Ratio = 36/12 = 3:1 (reads "3 to 1")
  • The driven gear spins 3× slower and has 3× more torque
  • Use case: tractor drivetrain, elevator motor, robot arm

Gear Ratio < 1 (Speed Increase)

  • Driven gear has FEWER teeth than driver
  • Driven gear rotates FASTER
  • But torque DECREASES
  • Example: 84-tooth driver → 60-tooth driven → Ratio = 60/84 = 0.714
  • The driven gear spins faster and has less torque
  • Use case: bicycle shifting up for downhill speed, fan blades

Hands-On with VEX Gears (POE_115)

  1. Mark one tooth on a 36-tooth gear with permanent marker. Mesh with an identical 36-tooth gear.
  2. Rotate the driver one full turn and observe the driven gear — same speed, opposite direction. Ratio = 1:1.
  3. Now mesh the 36-tooth driver with a 12-tooth driven gear. How many times does the small gear spin per driver revolution?
  4. Predict using the formula: Ratio = 12/36 = 1:3 → driven spins 3× per driver rotation. Test and verify.
  5. Measure both gears' diameters. Verify that Diameter_ratio = Teeth_ratio.
  6. Build and test 4 prescribed combinations. Record gear ratio and whether each optimizes for torque or speed.
Gear ratios multiply. If driver A meshes to gear B (ratio 3:1), and B meshes to gear C (ratio 2:1), the total ratio from A to C is 3 × 2 = 6:1. This is how compound gear trains achieve very large ratios.

Rotation Direction & Idler Gears

Opposite Direction

  • Two directly meshed gears ALWAYS rotate opposite to each other
  • Driver CW → Driven CCW
  • This is due to the teeth pushing against each other at the mesh point
  • Many applications require same-direction output (e.g., both wheels of a drive train turning the same way)

Idler Gear

  • Insert a third gear between driver and driven
  • The idler reverses direction twice: driver → idler (reverses), idler → driven (reverses again)
  • Net result: driver and final driven rotate in the SAME direction
  • Key fact: the idler does NOT change the overall gear ratio — only the final tooth counts of driver and driven determine the ratio
An idler gear is like a language interpreter — it changes how the gears communicate without changing what the final output does. Add an idler: same ratio, same-direction output.

Compound Gear Trains

  1. A compound gear train has multiple meshing stages in sequence
  2. The overall gear ratio = product of each stage's ratio
  3. Example: Stage 1 = 12-tooth driver → 36-tooth driven (ratio 3:1); Stage 2 = 12-tooth driver → 84-tooth driven (ratio 7:1)
  4. Overall ratio = 3 × 7 = 21:1
  5. A motor spinning at 1000 RPM through this train drives the output at 1000 / 21 ≈ 47.6 RPM
  6. And the output torque is 21× the motor's torque (minus friction losses)
Compound gear trains are how clock mechanisms, bicycle transmissions, and industrial gearboxes achieve large ratios in a compact package. Each stage independently contributes to the final ratio.

Challenge: "Go for Slow" (POE_115)

Design the Slowest Vehicle

  • Materials: VEX V5 Brain, one Smart Motor (Port 5), Limit Switch (Port A), Battery Pack, available gears
  • Goal: design and build the gear train that makes the slowest possible motorized vehicle
  • Download and run the "VEX Simple Motor Code" provided by your teacher
  • Measure your vehicle's travel time over a 30 cm course — longer time = lower score (slower = better!)
  • Record your gear combination, calculate the theoretical ratio, and compare to observed speed
There's a limit to how slow you can go. Too many gear stages add friction that eventually stalls the motor. Find the ratio that's slow enough to win without stalling. This is engineering — not just calculation.
