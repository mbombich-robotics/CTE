Meet Fran — Farm Engineering

Francesca (Fran) is a mechanical engineering graduate who wants to improve her farm's efficiency. Your challenge throughout this unit: design a mechanical device to solve one of Fran's farm problems.
  • Engineering Design Process (EDP) — the framework we use to solve problems systematically: Define → Research → Generate Ideas → Prototype → Test → Evaluate → Redesign
  • Criteria — the requirements a solution must meet. Example: "the device must lift 50 kg of hay"
  • Constraints — the limits on the solution. Example: "must cost under $200 and be built with available materials"
  • Why machines? In 1800, 90% of Americans worked in farming. Today it's 2% — because machines multiplied what one person can do.

The Six Simple Machines

Lever

A rigid bar that pivots on a fulcrum.

  • Seesaw
  • Crowbar
  • Scissors
  • Wheelbarrow

Inclined Plane

A flat surface tilted at an angle.

  • Ramp
  • Slide
  • Road up a hill
  • Escalator

Wedge

Two inclined planes joined at their thick ends.

  • Knife blade
  • Axe head
  • Doorstop
  • Zipper teeth

Screw

A spiraling inclined plane around a cylinder.

  • Wood screw
  • Jar lid
  • Spiral staircase
  • Bolt

Wheel & Axle

A large wheel fixed to a smaller axle; both rotate together.

  • Steering wheel
  • Water valve
  • Screwdriver
  • Bicycle pedal/sprocket

Pulley

A wheel with a grooved rim for a rope or cable.

  • Flagpole
  • Elevator
  • Crane
  • Window blinds
All complex machines — cars, combine harvesters, robots — are just combinations of these six simple machines. Identifying them in any machine is the first step to understanding how it works.

Simple Machines Inside a Combine Harvester

  • 🌾 Header / Cutter Bar — uses wedge action to cut crop stems at the base
  • 🔄 Auger / Screw Conveyor — a screw rotates to move cut grain horizontally into the machine
  • ⚙️ Threshing Cylinder — uses spinning wheels attached to axles; beats grain to separate seeds from stalks
  • 🪝 Elevator / Bucket Conveyor — uses a pulley system to lift separated grain up to the storage tank
  • 🎛️ Operator Controls / Steering — lever (throttle, selector) and wheel & axle (steering wheel)
  • 📐 Belt/Chain Drives — the combine uses inclined plane geometry in belt wedges to transfer power between rotating components
When agricultural engineer Cyrus McCormick developed the mechanical reaper in 1831, it reduced the labor to harvest one acre of wheat from 20+ person-hours to about 1 person-hour. That's mechanical advantage in action.

Mechanical Advantage — What Machines Do

A machine doesn't create force or energy. It changes the form or direction of an applied force — letting you do the same work with less effort (by moving farther), or in a different direction.
  • Effort Force (F_E) — the force you apply to the machine
  • Resistance Force (F_R) — the force the machine applies to the load
  • Mechanical Advantage (MA) = F_R / F_E — how many times the machine multiplies your force
  • MA > 1: machine multiplies force (but you move farther). Example: crowbar lifting a rock
  • MA < 1: machine multiplies speed/distance (but you need more force). Example: tweezers picking up something small
  • MA = 1: machine changes direction only, no force multiplication. Example: a single fixed pulley

Simple → Complex Machines

Simple Machine

  • Performs ONE mechanical function
  • One input motion → one output motion
  • Has one or at most a few moving parts
  • Examples: lever, screw, pulley wheel
  • Can calculate IMA from pure geometry

Compound Machine

  • Two or more simple machines connected together
  • Output of one simple machine feeds into the next
  • Can achieve large mechanical advantages
  • Examples: scissors (wedge + lever), bicycle (wheel/axle + lever + pulley), combine harvester (all six types)
The mechanical advantage of a compound machine = MA₁ × MA₂ × MA₃ × … Once you can analyze each simple machine in a compound system, you can analyze the whole system.

Ancient Engineering — Same Six Machines

  • The Great Pyramid of Giza (2560 BCE) — engineers used levers, inclined planes, wedges, and sleds with rollers (wheel & axle) to move 2.3 million blocks weighing 2.5–15 tons each
  • Ancient Egyptians had no engines or electricity — only human muscle + clever application of the six simple machines
  • The same principles that moved 15-ton granite blocks now move 15-ton hay bales (or 15-ton car bodies) using the same six simple machines — just with more automation
Simple machines were "simple" because they consisted of one piece — not because they're unsophisticated. They remain the foundation of all mechanical engineering today.

Activity: Build Three Simple Machines

Using Your VEX Kit

  • Identify 3 different simple machine types found in a real combine harvester
  • For each: document which simple machine type it is and what it does in the harvester
  • Construct each one using VEX V5 kit parts (beams, gears, pulleys, axles, rubber bands, etc.)
  • Measure or estimate the IMA of each machine you built
  • Share your three machines with another team — compare your designs and IMA values
Your engineering notebook should have: a sketch of each machine, which combine function it represents, the IMA calculation, and a comparison note from your peer review.
