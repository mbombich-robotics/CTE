VEX Robot
Torque vs. Traction

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1

Select a gear ratio that balances torque and traction for pulling performance

2

Distinguish accuracy from precision using repeated measurement data

3

Apply design-test-iterate: record results and justify a redesign decision

The Challenge

⚡ The Mission
Two teams. One rope. Your team's VEX robot must pull the other team's robot across the center line.
  • Robot must use a drivetrain (no dragging by hand)
  • Max 4 motors
  • Must fit within a defined starting box
  • No attaching to the field walls

Design Variables

Speed Side

  • High gear ratio (few teeth on driver)
  • Fast wheel speed
  • Low pulling torque
  • Good for: flat surface, light opponent

Torque Side

  • Low gear ratio (many teeth on driver)
  • Slow wheel speed
  • High pulling torque
  • Good for: heavy loads, traction battles
Key insight: Traction limits torque. A wheel that spins freely contributes nothing.

Know Your Targets

Precision

Your measurements are close together — repeatable — but may not hit the target value.

Accuracy

Your measurements are close to the true/target value — correct — but may vary.

In engineering, you need BOTH. A gear ratio you measured repeatedly as 3.0 (precise) but is actually 2.6 (not accurate) will lose the challenge.

Planning Your Robot

  1. Choose your wheel size — larger wheels = more speed, less torque
  2. Choose your gear ratio — calculate using GR = T_driven / T_driver
  3. Estimate your output torque — τ_out = τ_motor × GR
  4. Check traction — F_traction = μ × weight; if τ/r > F_traction, wheels spin
  5. Build, test, iterate — record data each run

Run Your Trials

Trial Gear Ratio Result Observation
1 __ : __ Win / Lose / Tie Wheels spun? Slipped?
2 __ : __ Win / Lose / Tie Grip better/worse?
3 __ : __ Win / Lose / Tie What changed?

Record in engineering notebook — data without context is useless.

Iteration

  • Lost? Ask: was it traction, torque, or strategy?
  • Won easily? Try a faster ratio — push your understanding
  • Wheels spinning? Increase weight on the robot or lower gear ratio
  • Pulling but losing ground? Opponent has more traction — redesign
  • Each iteration is data. No run is wasted.
Accuracy vs. Precision in practice: Did your prediction match your result? Analyze the gap.

Deliverables

  • Reflection: what would you do differently with more time?
