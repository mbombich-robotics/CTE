What Describes a Motor's Performance?

A motor's behavior can be described by four things, all plotted as functions of torque: speed, power, efficiency, and current. Together they form the motor's characteristic curves.
  • No-load speed (ω₀) — how fast the motor spins when nothing is attached (no load). Maximum speed, zero torque.
  • Stall torque (τstall) — the torque the motor can produce when it's barely turning (about to stop). Maximum torque, zero speed.
  • Speed vs. Torque is LINEAR: as you increase the load (torque), speed decreases in a straight line between (ω₀, 0) and (0, τstall)
  • Power vs. Torque is PARABOLIC: rises then falls, with a maximum in the middle
  • Maximum power occurs at exactly half the stall torque and half the no-load speed

Speed vs. Torque — The Linear Relationship

Formula: ω = (−ω₀ / τstall) × τ + ω₀  →  a straight line from ω₀ (at τ=0) down to 0 (at τ=τstall)

What the Graph Looks Like

  • X-axis: torque (τ), from 0 to τstall
  • Y-axis: speed (ω), from ω₀ down to 0
  • Straight negative slope
  • At zero torque (free running): max speed ω₀
  • At max torque (stalled): zero speed

What This Means for Engineers

  • You can't have max speed AND max torque simultaneously
  • Every gear selection shifts WHERE on this line the motor operates
  • Gear down (higher ratio): operating point moves toward high-torque, low-speed end
  • Gear up (lower ratio): operating point moves toward high-speed, low-torque end

Power: P = τ × ω

Substituting the speed equation into P = τ × ω gives: P = (−ω₀/τstall) × τ² + ω₀τ — a parabola.
  • At τ=0: P = 0 (no torque = no work done, even at max speed)
  • At τ=τstall: P = 0 (motor stalled = no movement = no work done)
  • The parabola peaks BETWEEN these extremes
  • Maximum power occurs at τ = τstall / 2 (exactly halfway between 0 and stall torque)
  • At maximum power: speed also equals ω₀ / 2 (halfway between 0 and no-load speed)
  • This is the Maximum Power Point (MPP) — the sweet spot for motor operation

Measuring Stall Torque

  1. Setup: Attach a Vernier Force Sensor to the VEX Gearbox Motor Winch cable. Prevent the output from rotating (create a stall condition by holding the wheel).
  2. Record the force (F, in Newtons or pounds) at the stall condition, and the moment arm distance (d, from the axle center to where the force is applied).
  3. Calculate stall torque: τstall = d × F⊥ (distance × perpendicular force component)
  4. Maximum power torque: τmaxPower = τstall / 2
  5. This gives you the target load torque for maximum power delivery

Gear Selection for Maximum Power

Gear Ratio = τout / τin — select the gear ratio so the motor's output torque matches τmaxPower for your application's load.
  1. Given: stalled motor force = 1.4 lb at d = 3 inches → τstall = 4.2 lb·in
  2. Maximum power torque: τmaxPower = 4.2 / 2 = 2.1 lb·in
  3. Load torque: a winch load of 0.2 lb at a 2-inch radius → τload = 0.4 lb·in
  4. Required gear ratio: GR = τload / τmaxPower = 0.4 / 2.1 = 0.19
  5. Find closest VEX gear combination: 12-tooth driver → 60-tooth driven: GR = 12/60 = 0.20 ≈ 0.19 ✓
  6. This gear combo makes the motor operate near its maximum power point for this load.

Lab: Motor Characterization (POE_116)

  1. Attach a "finger striker" (doubled-over tape) to the output wheel rim; loop the cable through the wheel spokes; attach force sensor
  2. Run motor at 6V. Count 20 revolutions while timing. Record elapsed time. Repeat 3 times.
  3. Measure current (I) and voltage (V) with multimeter during operation
  4. Calculate no-load speed: ω₀ = (20 revolutions) / (average time)
  5. Enter data into the provided Motor Power spreadsheet — it generates the characteristic curves
  6. From the graph: identify τmaxPower, ω at max power, and Pmax
The spreadsheet does the heavy math. Your job is accurate measurements (three trials for precision) and understanding what the resulting graphs mean for your application.

Connecting Motor Curves to Gear Selection

  • Undersized gear ratio (too little torque): motor runs fast but can't move the load; high speed, low torque, low power
  • Oversized gear ratio (too much torque): motor nearly stalls under normal conditions; high torque, very low speed, low power
  • Optimal gear ratio: motor operates near τstall/2 → maximum power delivered to load
  • This principle governs: bicycle gearing (shift to stay in the power band), car transmissions (torque converter + gears), industrial machinery (gearbox selection for the load)
You now have the full toolkit: measure the motor's stall torque → calculate τmaxPower → calculate required gear ratio → select the nearest available gear combination. This is real motor-drive system design.
