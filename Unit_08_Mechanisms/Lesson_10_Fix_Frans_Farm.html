Fran's Three Problems

🌾 Challenge 1 — The Hay Problem: Fran must lift 441 N (99 lb) of hay to a loft 3.5 m above the barn floor. Design a pulley system that a single person can operate with no more than 150 N of effort.
⚙️ Challenge 2 — The Mill Problem: The old grain mill wheel turns at 8 rpm. The grinding stone needs to spin at 32 rpm. Design a gear train to achieve this speed increase.
🔧 Challenge 3 — The Gate Problem: Design a four-bar linkage latch for the chicken coop gate that stays latched under wind load but opens with one hand.
Pick ONE challenge to engineer in depth. Your team will present to an investor panel.

Challenge 1 — The Hay Pulley

  • Required effort ≤ 150 N; load = 441 N
  • Minimum IMA needed: 441 / 150 = 2.94 → must use IMA ≥ 3
  • A compound pulley with 3 rope segments gives IMA = 3
  • Actual effort = Load / AMA; AMA = IMA × η
  1. Sketch your pulley arrangement — show all rope segments
  2. Count rope segments supporting the load (= IMA)
  3. Account for efficiency: assume η = 0.85 → AMA = 3 × 0.85 = 2.55
  4. Calculate actual effort: F_e = 441 / 2.55 = 173 N — still over! Add a 4th segment.
  5. With IMA=4, AMA=3.4 → F_e = 441/3.4 = 130 N ✓
More mechanical advantage = more rope to pull. Fran pulls more distance but with less force.

Challenge 2 — Mill Speed

  • Input: 8 rpm (waterwheel); Output: 32 rpm (grinding stone)
  • Speed ratio needed: 32/8 = 4.0 (speed increase)
  • Gear ratio = T_driven / T_driver → for speed increase, T_driver > T_driven
  • GR = 1/4 → driver has 4× the teeth of driven

Single-Stage Option

  • Driver = 80 teeth, Driven = 20 teeth
  • GR = 20/80 = 0.25
  • ω_out = 8 × 4 = 32 rpm ✓

Two-Stage Option

  • Stage 1: GR = 0.5 (double speed)
  • Stage 2: GR = 0.5 (double again)
  • Combined: 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 → same result, smaller gears
Compound gear trains let you achieve large ratios with gears that physically fit in the machine.

Challenge 3 — Gate Latch

  • The latch must: stay closed under 50 N wind load, open with ≤ 20 N hand force
  • Use a four-bar linkage with an over-center lock (like a toggle clamp)
  • Over-center: when the linkage passes slightly past the equilibrium point, wind load HELPS lock it further
  1. Sketch the four-bar configuration — identify ground, driver, coupler, output
  2. Mark the "over-center" position (where the coupler and driver are nearly collinear)
  3. Calculate mechanical advantage at latch point — the handle gets force amplification near over-center
  4. Test: apply 50 N to the gate — does it stay latched?
Over-center mechanisms are self-locking: the harder you push, the more locked they become.

Your Design Sprint

  1. Define — Write your criteria (must do) and constraints (must not exceed) for your chosen challenge
  2. Research — Review your notes from L8.1–L8.9; identify which mechanisms apply
  3. Ideate — Sketch 3 different solutions; no idea is wrong at this stage
  4. Select — Use a decision matrix to pick the best solution
  5. Build & Test — Construct prototype, measure actual performance vs. criteria
  6. Communicate — Prepare your investor pitch (see next slide)

The Investor Pitch

💼 Investor Panel Presentation You have 3 minutes to convince investors to fund your solution.
  • Problem (30 sec): What is Fran's problem? Why does it matter?
  • Solution (60 sec): Which mechanism did you choose and why? Show your design sketch.
  • Data (60 sec): Does your solution meet the criteria? Show the math.
  • Tradeoffs (30 sec): What did your design sacrifice? What would you improve?
Investors fund solutions, not ideas. Your math is your proof.
