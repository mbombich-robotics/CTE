Unit 8 — Mechanisms

Study how forces, motion, and mechanical advantage work in engineered systems. You'll analyze gears, linkages, levers, and the mechanisms inside the machines you use every day — then apply those principles to your own designs.

~5–6 Weeks Hands-On Lab 10 Lessons
Lesson 8.1 Week 1
Simple Machines & Engineering Design
  • The six types of simple machines and where they appear
  • Criteria and constraints in the design process
  • Simple machines inside a combine harvester
  • Activity: identify and build 3 simple machines from VEX kit
Live
Lesson 8.2 Week 1
Mechanical Advantage
  • Ideal Mechanical Advantage (IMA) — calculated from geometry
  • Actual Mechanical Advantage (AMA) — measured from forces
  • IMA formulas: lever (arm ratio), inclined plane (slope/height)
  • Why AMA is always less than IMA (friction)
Live
Lesson 8.3 Week 2
Work, Power & Efficiency
  • W = F × d — work measured in Joules
  • P = W / t — power measured in Watts
  • η = Eout / Ein — efficiency as a percentage
  • Lab: VEX motor winch measures input vs. output energy
Live
Lesson 8.4 Week 2
Pulley Systems
  • Fixed, movable, and compound pulley configurations
  • IMA = number of rope segments supporting the load
  • Tradeoff: more rope pulled = less force needed
  • Lab: Onshape pulley simulation + power calculations
Live
Lesson 8.5 Week 3
Gears & Gear Ratios
  • Gear ratio = teethdriven / teethdriver
  • Torque vs. speed tradeoff — gear up for speed, down for torque
  • Idler gears and rotation direction
  • Build: "Go for Slow" — design the slowest VEX vehicle
Live
Lesson 8.6 Week 3
Maximizing Power Output
  • Motor power curves: speed vs. torque vs. power
  • Finding the operating point that maximizes output power
  • How gear selection shifts the operating point
  • Lab: measure motor output at different loads
Live
Lesson 8.7 Week 4
Tug of War Challenge
  • Apply gear ratio and power concepts in a direct competition
  • Design a drivetrain that maximizes pulling force
  • Iteration: test, measure, and improve your design
  • Challenge: class Tug of War tournament
Live
Lesson 8.8 Week 4
Linkages
  • What linkages do: transmit and transform motion
  • 4-bar linkage: input link, coupler, output link, ground
  • Slider-crank: converts rotation to reciprocating motion
  • Activity: A-Door-Able Hens — design a chicken coop door mechanism
Live
Lesson 8.9 Week 5
Types of Motion
  • Rotary, linear, reciprocating, and oscillating motion
  • Mechanisms that convert between motion types
  • Real-world examples: pistons, cams, crankshafts, escapements
  • Activity: identify motion types in compound machines
Live
Lesson 8.10 Week 5–6
Fix Fran's Farm — Capstone
  • Unit capstone: design a mechanical device for Fran's farm
  • Apply simple machines, gears, linkages, and motion types
  • Build and test your design; iterate based on results
  • Present: how your mechanism works and how you'd improve it
Live
