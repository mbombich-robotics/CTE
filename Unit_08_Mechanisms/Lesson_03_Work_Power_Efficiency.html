Energy — The Currency of Engineering

A machine doesn't create energy. It converts energy from one form to another. Understanding those conversions lets you predict how well a machine will perform.
  • Potential Energy — stored energy: gravitational (raised object), elastic (compressed spring), chemical (battery), electrical (charged capacitor)
  • Kinetic Energy — energy of motion: any moving object has kinetic energy
  • Thermal Energy — heat: the "waste" form that friction produces
  • Electrical Energy — energy carried by moving electrons through a circuit (I × V × t)
  • Law of Conservation of Energy: energy is never created or destroyed, only converted. Total energy in = useful output energy + heat losses.

Work: W = F × d

Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance. Standing still holding a heavy box? You're tired, but you did zero work (no displacement).
  • W = work (Joules, J)
  • F = force applied in the direction of motion (Newtons, N)
  • d = distance the load moves (meters, m)
  • 1 Joule = 1 Newton·meter = 1 N·m
  • Work is only done if the force and displacement are in the same direction. A force perpendicular to motion (like a string holding a pendulum) does zero work.
  • Conversion from US units: 1 pound = 4.448 N; 1 foot = 0.305 m; 1 ft·lb = 1.356 J

Power: P = W / t

Power is the rate of doing work — how much work gets done per second. Same work, half the time = twice the power.

Mechanical Power

  • P = W / t (Watts = Joules per second)
  • 1 W = 1 J/s
  • Example: lifting a 500 N crate 2 m in 4 s → W = 1000 J → P = 250 W
  • Horsepower: 1 hp = 746 W (still used in motors and engines)

Electrical Power

  • P_electrical = I × V (Amperes × Volts = Watts)
  • This is power consumed or produced by an electrical component
  • A 60W bulb at 120V draws 0.5A
  • The VEX motor converts electrical power (I × V) to mechanical power (F × d / t)
The motor efficiency lab measures BOTH: input power (I × V) and output mechanical power (F × d / t). The ratio is efficiency.

Efficiency: η = E_out / E_in

No machine converts 100% of input energy into useful output. The rest becomes heat. Efficiency (η) tells you what fraction is useful.
  1. η = E_out / E_in (express as decimal) or η = (E_out / E_in) × 100% (express as percent)
  2. E_in (electrical): P_in × t = I × V × t (Joules)
  3. E_out (mechanical): W_out = F × d (Joules — the work done lifting the load)
  4. η is always between 0 and 1 (0% to 100%); never above 1 — that would violate conservation of energy
  5. Higher efficiency → less energy wasted as heat → cooler motor → longer life → lower operating cost

Worked Example (POE_113)

  1. Given: a motor lifts a 2 kg mass 0.30 m in 2 seconds. Motor draws 0.5 A at 6 V.
  2. E_out (mechanical): F = 2 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 19.62 N; W = F × d = 19.62 N × 0.30 m = 5.89 J
  3. E_in (electrical): E = I × V × t = 0.5 A × 6 V × 2 s = 6.0 J
  4. Efficiency: η = 5.89 / 6.0 = 0.98 → 98% (an idealized example; real VEX motor lab values typically run 30–60%)
  5. Mechanical power: P_mech = W / t = 5.89 / 2 = 2.95 W
  6. Electrical power in: P_elec = I × V = 0.5 × 6 = 3.0 W
  7. Record η, then repeat for different loads to see how efficiency changes with load.

Lab: VEX Gearbox Motor Winch (POE_113)

  • Equipment: VEX POE Gearbox Motor Winch, digital multimeter, stopwatch, hanging masses (200 g assemblies)
  • Measure E_in: set multimeter to measure current and voltage simultaneously (or sequentially) while motor runs
  • Measure E_out: attach hanging mass; mark start and end positions on cable 30.0 cm apart; time how long the motor takes to lift mass from mark to mark
  • Calculate: W_out = F × d (where F = mass × 9.81 m/s²); E_in = I × V × t
  • Compare: calculate η, then increase load and repeat. Does efficiency go up or down with more load?
Friction steals energy at every gear mesh, bearing, and cable bend. More load = more friction = lower efficiency? Or does the motor run more efficiently near its rated torque? You'll find out in the lab.

Why Efficiency Matters for Engineering

Energy Cost

  • A machine at 40% efficiency wastes 60 cents of every energy dollar
  • Industrial motors run 24/7 — even a 5% efficiency gain saves enormous energy costs
  • Electric vehicle motors are ~85–95% efficient; gasoline engines ~25–35%

Heat and Life

  • Low efficiency = lots of waste heat
  • Heat degrades materials, accelerates wear, melts lubricants
  • A hot motor doesn't just cost more to run — it fails sooner
  • Cooling systems (fans, heat sinks, liquid cooling) exist because engineers couldn't get efficiency high enough
The VEX motor is an energy converter. It converts electrical energy to mechanical energy. The efficiency you measure is a property of THAT motor at THAT load — not all motors, not all loads.
