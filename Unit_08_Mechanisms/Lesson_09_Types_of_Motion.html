Motion Conversion

Lesson 8.9 · Mechanisms Unit

Five classic mechanisms that convert one type of motion into another.

The Motion Menu

Rotational

Continuous spinning

  • Motors
  • Wheels
  • Gears

Oscillating

Back-and-forth rotation / arc

  • Pendulum
  • Rocker arm
  • Wiper blade

Reciprocating

Back-and-forth in a straight line

  • Piston
  • Sewing needle
  • Pump

Linear

One-direction straight-line motion

  • Conveyor belt
  • Slider
  • Door
Real machines almost always need to CONVERT between these — that's what the next five mechanisms do.

Rack & Pinion

  • Converts: Rotational → Linear
  • A circular gear (pinion) meshes with a flat toothed bar (rack)
  • Rotating the pinion slides the rack left or right
  • Direction of rotation = direction of linear travel

Examples

  • Steering rack in a car
  • CNC machine axis drive
  • Camera zoom lens
  • Height-adjustable desk

Key Equation

Linear travel per rotation = π × d (pinion diameter) × number of rotations

Every time you turn a car's steering wheel, you're using a rack and pinion.

Crank & Slider

  • Converts: Rotational → Reciprocating
  • A rotating crank arm is pinned to a connecting rod, which drives a sliding piston
  • The piston only moves in a straight line — the crank rotates continuously
  • This is how every gasoline engine works

Examples

  • Car engine pistons
  • Bicycle pedal → wheel (reverse: reciprocating → rotational)
  • Air compressor pump
  • Reciprocating saw

Key Insight

Crank length = stroke / 2. A 50 mm crank = 100 mm total piston stroke.

You can run it backward: push the piston to make the crank rotate — that's how a bicycle works.

Treadle Linkage

  • Converts: Oscillating → Rotational (or reverse)
  • A foot pedal (treadle) rocks back and forth, driving a flywheel via a connecting rod
  • The flywheel smooths out the pulsed input into continuous rotation

Classic Examples

  • Treadle sewing machine
  • Foot-powered lathe
  • Pump organ bellows

Modern Version

Elliptical exercise machines and some rowing machines use the same principle — oscillating human motion → smooth rotational output.

Before electric motors, the treadle was THE way to power tools with human foot energy.

Quick-Return Mechanism

  • Converts: Rotational → Reciprocating — but with unequal speeds in each direction
  • The cutting stroke (forward) is slow and powerful
  • The return stroke is fast (no work being done)
  • Saves time on the non-productive return

Examples

  • Shaper machine (metal cutting)
  • Power hacksaw
  • Slotting machine
  • Some packaging machines

Why It Matters

On a shaper, 60% of time is cutting stroke, 40% is fast return. Without quick-return, 50/50 — 20% slower overall.

Engineering isn't just about making things work — it's about making them efficient.

Cam & Follower

  • Converts: Rotational → Oscillating (or any custom output profile)
  • A shaped disk (cam) rotates; a follower rides on its surface
  • The cam profile defines the follower's motion — any shape you want
  • "One-way" means the follower only follows in one direction (usually spring-loaded)

Examples

  • Engine valve timing (every car engine!)
  • Music boxes
  • Automatic door locks
  • Printing press ink rollers
  • Washing machine cycle control

Cam Profile Design

Circular arc → smooth motion. Steep face → fast follower jump. Dwell region → follower holds position while cam rotates.

The shape of the cam IS the program. Engineers literally design custom motion by carving the right shape.
