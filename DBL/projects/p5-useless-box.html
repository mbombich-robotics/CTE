Design & Build Lab · Project 5

Useless
Box

Build a device that exists only to undo itself. Flip the switch — a mechanism reaches out, flips it back, and goes to sleep. Your job is to design the box, the lid, and the arm that makes it happen, then wire a two-switch circuit that controls motor direction and stopping. No code. No microcontroller. Just mechanical design and a circuit that does exactly one useless thing.

This project is reverse-engineered from a commercial kit. Your first task is to figure out how it works — then design your own version from scratch.

4 Weeks Mechanisms Circuits Individual

⛔ Original Design Only

No downloads from Thingiverse, Printables, or any model repository. The box body, lid, and actuator arm must be designed by you in Fusion 360.

Project Constraints

📦
Internal Clearance

The box interior must fit the motor, battery holder, both switches, and all wiring with room to close the lid. Measure your components before you model your box. A box that doesn't close is not a working box.

🔄
Arm Must Flip the Switch

The actuator arm must travel far enough — and at the right angle — to fully flip the DPDT toggle from ON to OFF. Partial contact doesn't count. If the switch doesn't flip, the circuit doesn't reverse, and the arm doesn't come back.

⚡
Motor Must Stop at Home

The SPST limit switch must break the circuit when the arm reaches home position. A motor that runs continuously against a physical stop will overheat and burn out. The limit switch is not optional — it is the reason the motor survives.

🖨️
Three Designed Parts

Box body, lid, and actuator arm are all original designs. Each must function as intended — not just look right. The hinge must pivot, the arm must sweep the correct arc, and the box must close completely when the arm is at home.

How the Circuit Works

⚡ The DPDT + Limit Switch Sequence

1
You flip the DPDT toggle to ON.

Current flows through the DPDT in its "forward" configuration. The motor spins in the forward direction, rotating the actuator arm out of the box.

2
The arm pushes the lid open and reaches the toggle.

The actuator arm sweeps through its range of motion, opening the lid as it goes. The arm makes contact with the toggle switch and flips it back to OFF.

3
DPDT flipped — motor reverses.

With the toggle back to OFF, the DPDT is now in its "reverse" configuration. Both motor terminals swap polarity simultaneously. The motor now spins in the opposite direction, pulling the arm back into the box.

4
Arm retracts, lid closes behind it.

The arm returns to home position. The lid swings closed — either by gravity, spring tension, or the arm geometry pulling it shut.

5
Limit switch breaks the circuit. Motor stops.

At home position, the arm contacts the SPST lever microswitch. This normally-closed switch opens, breaking the circuit. The motor stops. The box sits silent — waiting for the next flip.

🔁 Why the DPDT Is the Key

A DPDT (Double Pole Double Throw) switch controls two separate circuits at once. Each pole connects to one terminal of the motor. When the switch flips, both poles swap simultaneously — reversing which motor terminal is positive and which is negative. Direction changes with no additional wiring logic. One switch. Two circuits. Motor reversal.

The Range-of-Motion Challenge

📐 Sketch Before You Model — Three Events Must Happen in Order

  1. Event 1 — Lid opens: The lid must be open wide enough to allow the arm to pass through before the arm reaches the toggle switch. If the arm hits the lid before the lid is clear, nothing works. This happens early in the arm's rotation arc.
  2. Event 2 — Arm flips the toggle: At some point in the arc, the arm must contact and fully flip the DPDT toggle switch. The arm length, motor position, and toggle switch position all determine whether this is geometrically possible. This is the heart of the design.
  3. Event 3 — Arm returns home and stops: The limit switch must be positioned so the arm contacts it only at the fully-retracted home position — not mid-arc, and not past home. Its placement must be intentional.

Do this on paper first. Draw a side view of the box. Mark the motor shaft center, the hinge pin center, and the toggle switch. Draw the arm at home, at mid-arc, and at full extension. Confirm all three events happen in the right order before modeling anything. A geometry that doesn't work on paper will not work in plastic.

Your Designed Components

📦
Box Body

Houses the motor, battery holder, and limit switch. Must be large enough for component clearance, with mounting features (bosses, channels, or clips) to secure internals without relying on friction alone. Exterior styling is a design decision.

🔲
Lid

Hinged to the box body using integrated pin pivots — no screws or hardware. Must open far enough to allow arm clearance and close flush when the arm is at home. Lid material (PLA, PETG, or acrylic) is your choice.

🦾
Actuator Arm

Mounts to the motor shaft and sweeps the arc that opens the lid and flips the switch. Arm length and pivot angle are determined by your range-of-motion sketch. The motor-shaft interface must be designed to match your specific motor.

Materials

Teacher-Provided
Electrical Components & Filament
  • DC gear motor (hobby gearmotor, D-shaft)
  • DPDT panel-mount toggle switch
  • SPST lever microswitch (limit switch)
  • Battery holder (4×AA)
  • 22 AWG hookup wire (multiple colors)
  • PLA and PETG filament
Optional
Upgrades & Variations
  • Torsion spring — for lid return force if gravity alone is insufficient
  • Clear acrylic sheet — laser-cut and engrave a personalized lid (replaces the 3D printed lid)
  • Spray paint or vinyl wrap — exterior finish

All optional materials must be discussed with Mr. Bombich before use. Acrylic lid engravings are done as a class batch — get your design approved and in the queue on time.

Deliverables

D_P5A
Week 1–2
Planning
Design Brief & Range-of-Motion Sketch

Prove your design is geometrically feasible on paper before anything is modeled or printed. This is the document that shows your thinking, not just your result.

  • Problem statement: what is a useless box, and what is your design approach
  • At least 2 concept sketches showing exterior styling and general proportions
  • Decision matrix for at least one design decision (lid material, arm shape, box proportions)
  • Range-of-motion side-view sketch: motor shaft, hinge, toggle switch, and arm positions at Events 1, 2, and 3 all labeled
  • Component layout top-view sketch: how motor, battery holder, and switches fit inside the box
  • Fusion 360 screenshots of all three designed components (box body, lid, actuator arm)
D_P5B
Week 3
Fabrication
Assembled & Wired Box

Physical evidence of your assembled box with all components mounted and wired. The box must function — a box that is assembled but does not complete the full cycle does not receive credit for this deliverable.

  • Photo: all three printed components laid out before assembly
  • Photo: interior wiring with components mounted (lid open, taken from above)
  • Photo: closed box, exterior view
  • Photo or video: arm at its full extension (mid-cycle, arm flipping the switch)
  • If the first assembly did not work: photo of the failure point, written diagnosis, and revised component(s) with before/after photos
D_P5C
Week 4
Demo & Reflection
Demonstration & Reflection

The final test. Three consecutive successful cycles — no assists, no repositioning between cycles. Then the written reflection that shows you understood what you built.

  • Video or live demo: three complete cycles without stopping or adjusting
  • Reflection: describe two design decisions you made and why — what were the options, what did you choose, and what would you change
  • Reflection: explain in your own words how the DPDT controls motor direction
  • Reflection: what was the hardest part to get right, and how did you solve it
  • For Level 3: documentation of your iteration (before/after with explanation) or completed acrylic lid with laser engraving

Grading Levels

C
Level 1 — Working

A box that completes the full cycle. Arm extends, flips the DPDT, retracts, and stops when the limit switch is contacted. All three designed components are original Fusion 360 models. All three deliverables submitted. The box closes when the arm is at home.

B
Level 2 — Refined

Everything in Level 1, plus: range-of-motion sketch in the design brief with all three events labeled and geometrically accurate. Lid opens fully before the arm reaches the toggle — no collision. Wire management is clean: no exposed conductors, wires routed and secured. Exterior shows intentional design beyond a plain rectangular box.

A
Level 3 — Exceptional

Everything in Level 2, plus one of: (a) laser-engraved acrylic lid with a personalized design, submitted to the engraving queue on time; or (b) a documented design iteration — a component that was redesigned after testing, with before/after photos and a written explanation of what failed and why. Arm reliably flips the switch on every cycle without manual adjustment. Reflection explains all three mechanical events in the student's own words.
