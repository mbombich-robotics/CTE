Design & Build Lab · Project 1

Fidget Spinner

Design, model, print, and assemble a working fidget spinner from scratch using Fusion 360 and the Bambu Lab 3D printer. You'll model the MR128 bearing as a precision CAD reference part, design a spinner body that holds four bearings, and iterate your design until the spinner runs smoothly.

3 Weeks Unit 2 Fusion 360 FDM Printing 3 Deliverables

Parts You'll Model

C1
MR128 Bearing
Model the purchased bearing in Fusion 360 as a precision reference part. Your C2 pockets are sized from this model.
⌀12mm OD × ⌀8mm ID × 3.5mm
C2
Spinner Body
Three-arm body with four bearing pockets — one center, one per arm. Arm shape is your creative choice. Pocket diameter is set by C1.
3.5mm body · 4 × bearing pockets
C1+C2
F360 Assembly
Seat the four C1 bearings into the C2 pockets inside Fusion 360. Verify flush fit before printing. This is your digital proof-of-concept.
4 bearings seated · screenshots required
C3
Center Grip
Optional extension for early finishers. Design your own ergonomic grip — no spec, just make it comfortable to hold and iterate at least once.
extension · no design brief

Deliverables

D_P1A
Week 1
Planning
Design Brief & Concept Sketches

Before opening Fusion 360, define what you're building and sketch your ideas on paper. Use the EDP to frame the problem and plan your solution.

  • Problem statement (one sentence — who needs what and why)
  • Design statement (measurable criteria + constraints for your spinner)
  • At least 2 concept sketches of your C2 arm shape
  • Decision matrix selecting your chosen concept
  • Submitted to portfolio before moving to Fusion 360
D_P1B
Week 2
CAD Package
CAD Package — Models & Assembly

Screenshots documenting your completed C1, C2, and Fusion 360 assembly. All models must be fully constrained and saved before submitting.

  • Screenshot: C1 MR128 bearing — isometric view in Fusion 360
  • Screenshot: C2 spinner body — isometric view showing all four pockets
  • Screenshot: F360 assembly — isometric view, bearings seated in C2
  • Screenshot: F360 assembly — top-down view
  • All models fully constrained (no free geometry)
D_P1C
Week 3
Finished Product
Assembled Spinner & Iteration Log

Your printed and assembled spinner, proven to spin freely, with documented evidence of at least one design iteration.

  • Photo: assembled spinner held in hand (showing all bearings seated)
  • Photo: top-down view on a flat surface
  • Iteration log: at least one entry — what you observed, what you changed, why, and the result
  • Spinner must spin freely when held at the center bearing

Grading Levels

C
Level 1 — Replicate

Model C1 and C2 as specified in the lessons. Correct pocket diameter, 3.5mm body, four pockets, arm shape of your choice. Print, assemble, and demonstrate that the spinner works. Submit all three deliverables on time.

B
Level 2 — Modify

Complete Level 1, then apply a meaningful design modification — a different arm count, textured surfaces, filleted/chamfered edges, adjusted arm length for balance. Justify the modification in your iteration log with before/after results.

A
Level 3 — Redesign

Complete Level 2, then pursue a substantially original design: a unique arm geometry, integrated branding, multi-material print, or a C3 center grip. Document your design decisions and iteration with the same rigor as a professional engineering log.
