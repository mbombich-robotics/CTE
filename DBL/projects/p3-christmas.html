Design & Build Lab · Project 3

Christmas
Ornament & Gift

Design and fabricate a multi-color 3D printed Christmas ornament or small gift — entirely from scratch in Fusion 360. You'll reverse engineer an LED to model it accurately, build it into your design, and send a multi-color print to the Bambu. The best ones light up.

3 Weeks Multi-Color Print LED Integration Reverse Engineering Individual

⛔ Original Design Only

No imports from Thingiverse, Printables, MyMiniFactory, or any other model repository. Every surface, body, and feature must be modeled by you in Fusion 360. Submitting a downloaded model — even heavily modified — is an automatic zero.

Project Constraints

🎄
Theme: Christmas

Your piece must be a Christmas ornament or a small Christmas gift. The theme and intent should be obvious without explanation. Student's choice — ornament or gift.

⚖️
Filament < 200g

Your final print(s) must use fewer than 200g of filament combined. Prototyping material does not count. Verify in Bambu Studio before submitting your sliced file.

💡
Exactly 2 LEDs

Your design must incorporate exactly 2 LEDs. LED placement must be intentional — they should enhance the piece, not be taped on as an afterthought.

🎨
Multi-Color Required

Your final print must use 2 or more filament colors via the Bambu AMS. Single-color prints do not meet the project requirements regardless of quality.

Reverse Engineering the LED

🔬 No CAD file exists for this LED — you model it from the physical part

  1. Obtain your LED from the class supply. Handle it carefully — the leads bend easily.
  2. Measure the LED body: outer diameter, body length, lens dome height. Use digital calipers. Record all dimensions with units in your design brief.
  3. Measure the leads: length from base of body, spacing between leads (anode and cathode), lead diameter.
  4. Document the electrical spec: forward voltage (Vf) and forward current (If) from the class reference sheet or by testing.
  5. Model the LED as a component in Fusion 360 — body, dome, and leads as separate features. Dimension every sketch to match your measurements.
  6. Use the LED model as a reference when designing your cavity or mount in the main ornament/gift body. The pocket should hold the LED snugly without adhesive at Level 2+.

Materials

Required
PLA Filament — Multi-Color

Your primary material. Choose your colors intentionally — the color plan should be part of your design brief. Limit: <200g for the final piece. Prototyping filament (single-color, low infill) is provided separately and doesn't count toward your limit.

Optional — Level 2 & 3
Mixed Materials

Your design may incorporate additional materials for a more complex or refined piece. Examples: a laser-engraved wood placard (xTool), fabric, wire, or sheet metal accents. Affixing methods must be designed — glue joints, press fits, or rivets. Document your assembly method in D_P3A.

Deliverables

D_P3A
Week 1
Planning
Design Brief & LED Model

Define what you're making and prove you understand the LED before any design work begins. The LED model must be complete before you start modeling your ornament or gift.

  • Problem statement: who is this for, what does it need to do or say
  • At least 2 concept sketches of the ornament/gift with LED placement shown
  • Decision matrix confirming your chosen concept
  • LED measurements table: body diameter, body length, lens height, lead length, lead spacing, Vf, If
  • F360 screenshot of your completed LED model with dimensions visible
  • Color plan: which filament colors, what goes where, and why
  • If using mixed materials: assembly method sketched and justified
D_P3B
Week 2
CAD & Prototype
Model, Slicer Setup & Prototype

Your complete CAD model, sliced and ready to print, with a prototype proving the LED fits. Due before your final print is submitted to the queue.

  • F360 screenshot: final model in isometric view showing all features
  • F360 screenshot: LED model placed in or near its cavity to show fit intent
  • If multi-part design: F360 assembly screenshot with joints visible
  • Bambu Studio screenshot: color-painted model showing all color regions
  • Bambu Studio screenshot: slicing preview confirming filament use <200g
  • Prototype photo: printed test piece (single color, low infill is fine) with LED physically test-fitted
D_P3C
Week 3
Finished Product
Final Product & Iteration Log

Your finished, lit piece — ready to give as a gift or hang on a tree. Two photos required so both the print quality and the LED effect are visible.

  • Photo: finished piece in normal light — shows print quality and color accuracy
  • Photo: finished piece with LEDs on in a dim environment — shows the lighting effect
  • If mixed materials: photo showing the assembly/affixing detail
  • Iteration log: at least one change made after the prototype (design, fit, color, or assembly) with before/after description
  • Filament weight confirmed: actual weight of finished piece documented

Grading Levels

C
Level 1 — Complete

A finished, original multi-color 3D print that is recognizably a Christmas ornament or gift. 1–2 filament colors. 2 LEDs installed. No assembly required. All three deliverables submitted on time with an iteration log entry.

B
Level 2 — Refined

Complete Level 1, plus: 3 or more filament colors used intentionally. LED cavity modeled to hold the LEDs without adhesive (press fit). Either a multi-part design with a simple assembly, or additional material incorporated (laser-engraved wood, etc.). Iteration log shows a meaningful design change with evidence.

A
Level 3 — Exceptional

Complete Level 2, plus: multiple modeled parts with a fully constrained F360 assembly (joints, mates, no free degrees of freedom). High design complexity — compound curves, detailed features, or mechanical function. Exceptional print quality and color execution. The finished piece is a genuinely excellent product you'd be proud to give as a gift.
