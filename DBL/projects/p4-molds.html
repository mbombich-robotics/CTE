Design & Build Lab · Project 4

Silicone
Mold Making

Design a product in Fusion 360, build the mold around it, pour Smooth-On Mold Star 15A silicone, and cast soap or wax you can actually use. The mold is the deliverable — if it doesn't release cleanly, it doesn't grade.

The class default is soap. If you want to make a candle instead, talk to Mr. Bombich first — candles require a different pour process. Taking your mold home to cast candy or popsicles? Go for it, the silicone is food-safe.

4 Weeks Silicone Pour Soap & Wax Individual

⛔ Original Design Only

No imports from Thingiverse, Printables, or any model repository. The positive — the object your mold will cast — must be designed by you in Fusion 360. Downloading a soap bar model and molding it is an automatic zero.

Project Constraints

🧼
Volume Requirement

Your mold cavity must hold at least the volume of a hotel soap bar (~30 mL). Measure the cavity volume using Fusion's Inspect → Measure tool and document the result in your design brief. Tiny, low-effort designs that don't meet this threshold do not satisfy Level 1.

📐
Draft Angles Required

All vertical walls on the positive must include at least 2–3° of draft. Without draft, silicone grips the positive and won't release. Check every face before printing. Your partner review must confirm draft is present.

🏭
Mold Must Release

If the silicone traps the positive and cannot be separated without destroying the mold, you must redesign and re-pour. A mold that doesn't release does not receive a grade until it works. Plan your draft angles and avoid undercuts.

🖨️
Print Material by Mold Type

One-part silicone molds: print in PLA — the mold box and positive are one print. Two-part rigid molds (Level 3): print in PETG — PLA will deform at soap and wax pour temperatures. PETG requests go in the printer queue labeled with your name and "PETG."

Designing the Mold Assembly

🔬 Designing the Mold Assembly in Fusion 360

  1. Design your product (the positive) first — this is the object your soap or wax will become. Model it as if it were the final product. Surface detail, text, and relief work all transfer to the cast.
  2. Add draft to every vertical wall — select each face and apply a 2–3° taper toward the open top of the mold. Do this before building the mold box around it.
  3. Check for undercuts — any feature that overhangs or is wider at the bottom than the top will trap the silicone. Silicone is flexible so mild undercuts are OK; severe ones are not. When in doubt, redesign.
  4. Build the mold box around the positive — walls must be at least 10 mm thick on all sides and at the base. The top is open. The positive sits at the bottom, attached to the base (model it as part of the box — do not try to glue a separate positive in place).
  5. Measure your cavity volume — use Inspect → Measure → Volume on the void space inside the mold box. Must be ≥ 30 mL. Document the number in your design brief.
  6. For Level 3 (two-part rigid mold): design both halves as separate bodies in the same Fusion file. Add alignment pins (cylindrical, 4 mm diameter, 8 mm tall — male on one half, pocket on the other). Add a gate channel (8 mm diameter, top-center) and a vent (3 mm, highest point of the cavity). Apply draft to all cavity faces. Print in PETG.

🔍 Partner Design Review — Required Before Printing

Before you submit anything to the print queue, a classmate reviews your Fusion model and signs off. This is how it works in every professional engineering environment — someone else catches what you miss.

Reviewer checklist:

  • Do all vertical walls have visible draft (at least 2–3°)?
  • Are there any undercuts that would prevent the silicone from releasing?
  • Are embossed or engraved features at least 1 mm deep/tall? (shallower features won't cast reliably)
  • Is the cavity volume documented and ≥ 30 mL?
  • For Level 3: do both halves have alignment pins? Is the gate at the lowest point? Is there a vent?

The reviewer's name goes in the design brief. Both students are responsible — reviewer included.

Materials

Required
Smooth-On Mold Star 15A

Teacher-supplied. Food-safe platinum silicone, shore hardness 15A (soft and flexible). Mix ratio 1:1 by volume. Pot life approximately 15 minutes — have your mold ready before you open the containers. Cure time 24 hours at room temperature. Do not rush the cure. Your mold will be poured as a class during a designated pour day.

Optional — Level 3
Mold Release Spray

Required for two-part rigid PETG molds. A light coat on each half before pouring prevents the wax or soap from bonding to the plastic. Teacher has a can — ask before your pour day. One-part silicone molds don't need release agent.

Grading Levels

C
Level 1 — Complete

A one-part silicone mold that releases cleanly. Cavity volume meets the hotel bar minimum. The positive has at least one piece of intentional surface artwork, text, or relief — not a plain block. All three deliverables submitted. Partner review signed.

B
Level 2 — Refined

Everything in Level 1, plus: draft angles intentionally designed and documented (not accidental). Surface design shows genuine effort — composition, detail, and feature clarity transfer to the cast. The finished soap bar or candle is giftable quality. Iteration log shows a real design change with evidence.

A
Level 3 — Exceptional

Everything in Level 2, plus: two-part rigid PETG mold with alignment pins, gate, and vent correctly positioned. The cast product is fully three-dimensional — not flat-backed. Wax candle cast from the rigid mold. Both halves register cleanly with no flash at the parting line. Documentation traces every major design decision from brief to final result.
