Design & Build Lab · Project 2

Christmas
Ornament & Gift

Design and fabricate a 3D printed holiday ornament or small gift, entirely from scratch in Fusion 360. You'll reverse engineer an LED, model it accurately, and build it into your design so the finished piece lights up.

The class theme is Christmas. If you celebrate a different holiday this time of year — Hanukkah, Kwanzaa, Eid, Diwali, or anything else — design for that instead. Same engineering requirements, your tradition.

Due Fri Dec 11 60 Points LED Integration Reverse Engineering Individual

⛔ Original Design Only

No imports from Thingiverse, Printables, MyMiniFactory, or any other model repository. Every surface, body, and feature must be modeled by you in Fusion 360. Submitting a downloaded model — even heavily modified — is an automatic zero.

Project Constraints

🎄
Theme: Holiday

Default theme is Christmas — ornament or small gift. If you celebrate a different holiday, design for that instead. Either way, the theme and intent must be obvious without explanation.

⚖️
Filament: 100 g max

Your finished project must use 100 g of filament or less. Check the weight in Bambu Studio before you print, and report what you used. Test prints don't count toward the 100 g, but print them in one color. This is a hard limit: if you think you need more, talk to Mr. B before you print and explain why.

💡
1 to 3 LEDs

At least one LED is required, and you may use up to three. Place them on purpose: they should be part of the design, not taped on as an afterthought.

🎁
One Item or More

You work alone. You may make more than one item, as long as everything together stays within 100 g, or Mr. B has given you permission for more.

Reverse Engineering the LED

🔬 No CAD file exists for this LED — you model it from the physical part

  1. Obtain your LED from the class supply. Handle it carefully — the leads bend easily.
  2. Measure the LED body: outer diameter, body length, lens dome height. Use digital calipers. Record all dimensions with units in P2.0.
  3. Measure the leads: length from base of body, spacing between leads (anode and cathode), lead diameter.
  4. Document the electrical spec: forward voltage (Vf) and forward current (If) from the class reference sheet or by testing.
  5. Model the LED as a component in Fusion 360 — body, dome, and leads as separate features. Dimension every sketch to match your measurements.
  6. Use the LED model as a reference when designing your cavity or mount in the main ornament/gift body. The pocket should hold the LED snugly without adhesive at Level 2+.

Materials

Required
PLA Filament

Your primary material. One color or several: choose on purpose and explain your choice under design intent in your scope document. Limit: 100 g for the finished project.

Optional — Level 2 & 3
Mixed Materials

Your design may incorporate additional materials for a more complex or refined piece. Examples: a laser-engraved wood placard (xTool), fabric, wire, or sheet metal accents. Affixing methods must be designed — glue joints, press fits, or rivets. Describe how the parts join in your scope document.

Grading Levels

The minimum for a C: an assembly of at least two items, 100 g of filament or less, and a complete scope document. Each step up asks for more thought, not more material.

Each of the six rows is worth 10 points, 60 in all: A = 10, B = 8.5, C = 7.5, Below C = 5, not turned in = 0.

C
Minimum
  • Assembly: Two items joined into one ornament (for example a cookie ornament with an LED).
  • Scope document: Scope, design intent and recipient are each addressed in a few sentences.
  • Design intent: One design choice is named and explained.
  • Fit to recipient: A recipient is named.
  • Filament: Within 100 g.
  • Finish: Complete and usable, with visible flaws.
B
Refined
  • Assembly: Two or more items joined and working, with a joint you chose on purpose.
  • Scope document: All three parts are present and mostly specific.
  • Design intent: At least two choices are explained and visible in the ornament.
  • Fit to recipient: The design suits the recipient in an obvious way.
  • Filament: Within 100 g, and the amount used is reported.
  • Finish: Mostly clean, with small flaws that do not affect use.
A
Exceptional
  • Assembly: Two or more items joined in a way that is planned and clearly works, such as a light that is hidden, held, or powered neatly.
  • Scope document: All three parts are clear, specific, and match what you built.
  • Design intent: Several choices are explained, and the finished ornament shows them.
  • Fit to recipient: The design clearly suits a named person or group, and you can say how.
  • Filament: Planned amount is close to the amount used, with little waste.
  • Finish: Clean print, parts fit well, and it is ready to hang or give.

Below C Assembly: Only one item, or the parts do not stay together. Scope document: A part is missing, or the document is not turned in. Design intent: No reasons are given for how it looks or works. Fit to recipient: No recipient is named. Filament: Over 100 g without permission. Finish: Unfinished or not usable as an ornament.
