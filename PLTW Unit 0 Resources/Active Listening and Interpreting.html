Active Listening and Interpreting

Communication is a two-way street. To communicate—whether through music, an emergency broadcast, a speech, or a conversation—someone must receive and interpret the communication. Interpreting communication does not mean hearing.

Hearing is the physical ability to perceive sound, but active listening and interpreting is a skill that allows you to process and make sense of what is communicated. This skill takes practice but will pay off in your understanding and relationships.

Benefits

  • Improves your personal and professional relationships and earns you the trust and respect of your peers.
  • Helps you better understand what is said.
  • Leads to better solutions in your collaborative work.

Practice

Clear your mind of distractions. Attend to what is being said and how it is said. Notice the speaker's tone of voice, facial expressions, and body language. If you catch yourself becoming distracted, remind yourself to focus on the speaker.

Keep an open mind. Concentrate on what is being said and not on characteristics of the speaker or the environment. Be open to new ideas and new perspectives.

Show your interest and concern with eye contact, head nodding, facial expressions, leaning forward, and short verbal responses.

At appropriate times, summarize and paraphrase what you are hearing. Ask questions to be sure you understand correctly.
